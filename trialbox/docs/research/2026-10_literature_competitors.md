## 0. Corrections and gaps in the existing landscape (read this first)

1. **Lind "TRIAGE, JCO OP June 2026, 94.2 % criterion agreement, 806 patients, 168 trials" is not verifiable.** Advocate Health's own release says a retrospective study at Atrium Health Wake Forest Baptist covered "over 1,700 eligibility criteria" with "more than 94 % agreement with clinical reviewers" and that the platform "still requires human review" (https://www.advocatehealth.org/news/advocate-health-partners-with-ai-startup-to-connect-more-cancer-patients-to-clinical-trials; https://www.hcinnovationgroup.com/analytics-ai/artifical-intelligence-machine-learning/news/55380471/advocate-health-partners-with-startup-lind-on-clinical-trial-matching). A PubMed search of *JCO Oncology Practice* for AI eligibility/screening papers since June 2025 returns one unrelated 2024 record (PMID 39106420), and a search for "TRIAGE" + Atrium + AI eligibility returns nothing [V]. Treat the journal citation, the 94.2 %, the 98.7/97.6 % sensitivity/specificity and the 806/168 counts as **[Unverified]** until a DOI is produced. The existing docs' acceptance targets lean on this number; they should lean on the UTSW and RECTIFIER numbers below instead.
2. **Epic did not ship "AI chart-vs-criteria checks in the November 2025 release."** Epic's own "Epic & Generative AI" roadmap (last updated 24 April 2025) lists "Evaluate patients for clinical trials using eligibility data and the chart" as *in development*, target "2026", with no quarter; "Identify cancer staging data from notes" is the Q1 2026 item (https://scrmc.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/09/Epic-Generative-AI-1.pdf) [V]. Forward CTMS "first phases available starting in 2026, continued rollout in 2027" (https://www.healthcareitnews.com/news/himss26-epic-highlight-no-code-agent-factory-and-other-ai-advances) [R]. Separately, OpenAI connected ChatGPT for Healthcare to Epic environments in September 2026 (cited chart answers; a public-data plugin covering ClinicalTrials.gov and PubMed; 4,363 physician ratings, 99.1 % "safe") (https://shellypalmer.com/2026/09/openai-wires-chatgpt-into-epics-ehr/) [R]. That is the more consequential EHR-vendor move for trial matching, and it is absent from the landscape.
3. **The "neuro-symbolic multi-agent, 3,804 patients, F1 0.82" study (ref 32) is Massive Bio's platform**, announced by the company on 9 April 2026 (https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20260409537626/en; https://www.galvnews.com/massive-bio-publishes-landmark-prospective-study-demonstrating-ai-driven-clinical-trial-matching-at-scale-in/article_ac6ba005-73e2-56ed-b2bd-6749867f168f.html) [V]. Massive Bio is a competitor the landscape omits entirely, and it entered South Korea in September 2026 via Intralink, claiming 200,000 patients matched to 19,000 trials across 17 markets (https://biospectrumasia.com/news/120/28392/us-health-tech-company-massive-bio-expands-asia-footprint-with-south-korea-market-push.html) [Vendor].
4. **The largest real-world LLM pre-screening deployment published to date is missing:** UT Southwestern pre-screened **39,182 patients across 26 studies (21 oncology, 5 non-oncology)** from Oct 2024 to Sep 2025 with a RAG pipeline on GPT-4o → GPT-4o-mini → GPT-4.1-mini in Azure; criterion-level accuracy 0.94 (95 % CI 0.92–0.96), sensitivity 0.98, specificity 0.81, PPV 0.95, F1 0.97 on 5,096 coordinator-reviewed criteria; cost about **$0.12 per patient**; enrollment and time savings **not measured** (https://www.medrxiv.org/content/10.64898/2026.03.20.26348890v1.full) [V, preprint]. Its failure modes (a Tier-1 hard gate that blocks downstream criteria, "timing errors" where old notes look current, scanned external records excluded, 0–180-day look-back) are exactly TrialBox's design risks.
5. **"Agents" and "feasibility AI" have no peer-reviewed evidence base in 2026.** No peer-reviewed study of an LLM agent acting as a coordinator, of LLM-generated feasibility questionnaires, or of agentic feasibility exists; everything is vendor material (ConcertAI, Saama, Salesforce Agentforce, Research Grid, Medidata, Truveta). The 2026 narrative review on AI readiness in trial operations concludes that patient-trial matching is the strongest-evidenced use case and even it "reached only moderate maturity, being evaluated mainly retrospectively or in simulated scenarios" (Healthcare 2026, PMID 42651288) [V].
6. **TrialPanorama does not benchmark patient–trial matching.** The 2025/2026 TrialPanorama tasks are study search, screening, evidence summarisation and trial-design tasks; the patient-matching F1 figure (+14.4 %) comes from the earlier **Panacea** paper, which used the same name for a different benchmark (https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.16097; https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.11007) [V]. MedHELM has no trial-matching task that could be found [Unverified].
7. **MedCPT is not covered**, yet it is the obvious domain retriever for criterion-to-note retrieval: contrastively trained on about 255 M PubMed click-log pairs, with a public retriever and cross-encoder (https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.00589; https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12478430/) [V]. No 2025–2026 evaluation of MedCPT on patient-to-trial retrieval was found; it is a cheap ablation TrialBox should run against bge-m3/Qwen3-Embedding.
8. **The best public n2c2-2018 number with an open model is now 89.05 % micro-F1 (MedGemma + retrieval)**, still below the 0.9163 pre-LLM hybrid record (https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.05190; https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.11114; https://scholar.hit.edu.cn/en/publications/a-unified-machine-reading-comprehension-framework-for-cohort-sele/) [V]. "Open models match GPT-4" is true criterion-by-criterion in some studies and false on the standard benchmark.
9. **Local small models on CJK notes are weaker than the docs imply.** A locally deployed LLaMA-3.1-8B with QLoRA reached only **micro-F1 0.49 (strict) / 0.59 (tolerant)** on hierarchical structuring of Japanese SOAP notes (https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-026-58622-8) [V]; by contrast a QLoRA Gemma-2-27B reached macro-F1 0.906 on nine infectious-disease fields in Japanese primary-care notes, and the 2B variant 0.822 (https://formative.jmir.org/2026/1/e84974) [V]. Task shape, not language, decides; 8B is not enough for schema-heavy extraction.
10. **Korea and Japan startups are under-described.** Japan: Buzzreach (StudyWorks, Nara Medical University regional recruitment infrastructure, IIT support Feb 2026) [R]; Ubie × Osaka University Hospital AMED pilot on generative AI for trial documents and patient recruitment [R]; MDV was **delisted from TSE Prime on 24 April 2026** and holds a 57.56 M-patient EMR database with a feasibility/site-selection service (https://www.mdv.co.jp/news/uploadfile/docs/ce0b1a98a6bcf9a5eff5e9843b4136c7bd81d0a3.pdf; https://digitalpr.jp/r/65233) [V]. Korea: a 710 bn-won (~$480 M) five-year plan announced at Medical Korea 2026 pairs AI recruitment with DCT protocols (https://www.geneonline.com/medical-korea-2026-south-korea-deploys-480-million-to-build-a-global-clinical-trial-hub/) [R]; no Korean hospital LLM pre-screening deployment was found; the only LLM clinical-ops trial registered is a psychiatric intake tool at Gangnam Severance (NCT07875816) [V].
11. **Taiwan: two new facts.** China Medical University Hospital and AstraZeneca Taiwan signed a trial-start-up MOU on 30 April 2026 that explicitly cites AI for trial design and execution (https://www.ctee.com.tw/news/20260430701918-430504) [R]; TACTC signed MOUs with six strategic partners on 5 October 2026 (https://health.tvbs.com.tw/nutrition/366474) [R]. The MOHW national compute centre and cross-border federated-learning platform launched 28 January 2026 with all hardware in Taiwan, and ITRI's FHIR coding tool reached 91.3 % coding-recommendation accuracy on 12,756 records at three medical centres (https://www.ithome.com.tw/news/173830; https://geneonline.news/mohw-launches-ai-validation-with-fhir/) [R]. Still no Taiwanese LLM pre-screening product.
12. **Azure AI Health Insights Trial Matcher:** an Azure retirement notice names "Clinical Trials Matcher and Radiology Insights" as being retired; the date was not retrievable (https://learn.microsoft.com/azure/azure-health-insights/overview) [Unverified date]. The landscape's "stalled" is now "being retired."
13. **Veeva's clinical agents (August 2026, 26R2) and Clinical Data agents (December 2026) could not be confirmed as shipped** (https://www.veeva.com/eu/resources/veeva-ai-agents-to-be-released-across-all-veeva-applications/; https://www.clinicaltrialvanguard.com/?p=25430) [Unverified]. Veeva eSource inside SiteVault (FHIR-based EHR→EDC) was presented April 2026 (https://www.appliedclinicaltrialsonline.com/view/veeva-new-approach-scalable-esource-research-sites) [R] and is a free-to-site competitor for TrialBox's eCRF pre-fill.
14. **Reify Health / OneStudyTeam (StudyTeam)** is the de facto site-side enrollment tracker at 1,800–2,700 sites and has a 2026 "Referral Partner Interface" (https://yespress.io/reify-health; https://www.fiercebiotech.com/cro/amgen-taps-reify-health-for-better-trial-enrolment) [R]. It is absent from the matrix although it occupies the "enrollment tracking" cell TrialBox marks as core.
15. **Prompt injection via the chart is a new, named failure mode**: referral letters, scanned external reports and patient messages are untrusted input to any LLM reading an EHR (Ann Biomed Eng 2026, PMID 42745101) [V]. Neither spec nor landscape mentions it.

## 1. Literature 2024–2026

### 1.1 LLM matching and eligibility

**TrialGPT and successors.** TrialGPT (Nat Commun 2024) remains the reference: 87.3 % criterion-level accuracy on 1,015 pairs, 42.6 % screening-time reduction, synthetic patients (https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-024-53081-z) [V]. **TrialGPT 2.0** (arXiv 2609.01202, Sept 2026): top-10 contained a clinician-recommended trial in about 91 % of 288 retrospective multicentre cases; screening time −55 %; 21.7 s per run over >25,000 active US trials; in a six-month prospective run (Feb–Jul 2026) in the University of Illinois precision-oncology tumour board, "expanded access" of 90.9 % is defined narrowly as cases where the routine workflow produced **no** final recommendation and TrialGPT 2.0 contributed at least one trial the clinicians kept; false negatives are not reported (https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.01202; https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/research/trialgpt/) [V, preprint]. Code for TrialGPT is public (https://github.com/ncbi-nlp/TrialGPT) but the licence was not checked [Unverified]. **Local TrialGPT at UT Health San Antonio** (JAMIA 2026): sensitivity 81.8 %, specificity 97.8 %, PPV 75 %, finding 81.8 % vs 36.4 % of truly eligible patients against manual screening (https://doi.org/10.1093/jamia/ocag006) [V].

**TrialMatchAI** (Nat Commun 2026; arXiv 2505.08508): fully open-source, fine-tuned open LLMs, hybrid lexical+dense retrieval, criterion-level chain-of-thought; 92 % of oncology patients had a relevant trial in the top 20 and >90 % criterion-level accuracy on expert review; models, synthetic patients and code on Zenodo (https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-026-70509-w; https://zenodo.org/records/15045515; https://ous-research.no/home/ous/news/26758) [V]. The authors say it "still needs validation across larger and more diverse cohorts." **LLM-Match** (arXiv 2503.13281) fine-tunes open models with RAG and reports best-in-class on n2c2, SIGIR 2016, TREC 2021/2022 [V, preprint]. **MatchMiner-AI** (Dana-Farber/Mayo) releases synthetic training data, weights and inference tools for genomically guided matching (https://themoonlight.io/review/matchminer-ai-an-open-source-solution-for-cancer-clinical-trial-matching; https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC9537311/) [R]. **Panacea** (arXiv 2407.11007) is a trial-domain foundation model; its matching gain (+14.42 % F1 on two datasets) is the only "TrialPanorama" matching number [V, preprint].

**Retrieval over long records.** Evidence-localisation study (arXiv 2604.05190): three ways of handling long narratives (native context, NER-based extractive compression, criterion-conditioned dynamic retrieval) on n2c2 2018; best = MedGemma + retrieval, 89.05 % micro-F1 [V, preprint]. Tempus (AACR 2026 abstract) reports that agentic, reasoning-guided iterative retrieval improves eligibility classification from notes over single-pass retrieval (https://www.tempus.com/publications/reasoning-guided-retrieval-improves-oncology-trial-eligibility-matching-from-clinical-notes/) [Vendor, abstract only]. A myeloma study on 811 patients (44,962 documents) found a skill-library-guided agent reached 80.1 % concordance where iterative RAG (75.7 %) and full-context input (75.8 %) had converged, with the advantage widening on the longest records (npj Precis Oncol 2026, PMID 42875467) [V]. Together these say: for long longitudinal records, dynamic criterion-conditioned retrieval beats both naïve chunking and "stuff the whole chart in".

**RECTIFIER / JAMA RCT** remain the only randomised evidence: eligibility determination 20.4 % vs 12.7 %, enrollment 1.6 % vs 0.9 %, no demographic shift (https://doi.org/10.1001/jama.2024.28047; https://doi.org/10.1056/AIoa2400181) [V]. **OncoLLM/PRISM** (npj Digit Med 2024): fine-tuned Qwen-1.5-14B "matches qualified doctors"; secondary cost figures of $0.17 vs $6.18 per patient–trial pair come from the UCSF systematic review (https://doi.org/10.1038/s41746-024-01274-7; https://arxiv.org/pdf/2509.19327) [V/R]. **Yale CTPM** (JCO CCI 2026; PMID 41512229) is confirmed as published; the 98,348/825/117 funnel numbers were not re-verifiable from the abstract in this pass [Unverified counts].

**Massive Bio prospective study** (ESMO RWD Digit Oncol 2026; 3,804 patients; F1 0.82 vs 0.47 zero-shot GPT-4; per-patient time 120 → ~30 min) is vendor-authored [V, with conflict of interest].

**Open-weight deployments.** Western University (Canada) evaluated **14 open-weight models** on 219 nephrology clinic notes against six kidney trials; "when each criterion is assessed one at a time, several locally deployable open-weight models prescreen with accuracy comparable to a physician reading the same notes" (work in progress, no numbers) (https://www.schulich.uwo.ca/epibio/undergraduate/year_4_research_project/adam_klotz.html) [Unverified]. A German group extracted mutational eligibility criteria from registries with **local LLMs plus HGNC rule validation** (Stud Health Technol Inform 2026, PMID 42752480) [V]. A JCO Precis Oncol 2026 paper benchmarked eight LLMs including open models on 814 cell-surface-target trials for target extraction (PMID 41785439) [V]. The Kindai/NTT/Chugai study (June 2026–March 2027) compares rule-based (Python/SQL), tsuzumi-2 LLM and hybrid extraction against physician/CRC judgment; no results yet (https://group.ntt/jp/newsrelease/2026/06/30/260630a.html; https://www.nttdata.com/global/ja/news/topics/2026/063001) [V]. Fujitsu/Nagoya/Gifu (reported May 2025, 1,800 breast-surgery patients): ~90 % recall on structuring; **27 of 42 AI-found candidates confirmed eligible** across three past trials; selection time cut by about two-thirds (https://www.thers.ac.jp/news/upload/d8e794920b1fe5c825cefb3f059d215c.pdf; https://it.impress.co.jp/articles/-/27902) [V]. That 64 % PPV on real Japanese charts is the most relevant Asian number.

**Smaller 2026 items.** A single-patient pancreatic-cancer zero-shot study documents qualitative reasoning errors (https://www.medrxiv.org/content/10.64898/2026.07.06.26354647) [V, preprint]. "Patient2Sentence" compresses oncology EHRs into standard sentences; 94.7 % concordance (κ 0.83) on 75 synthetic records (medRxiv 2025.11.14.25340276) [Unverified, not located directly]. An ASCO 2026 abstract on mCODE-aligned extraction reports 98 % tumour-type but 69–77 % relapse/resectability concordance (https://www.citedrive.com/en/discovery/expanding-global-access-to-oncology-trials-mcode-aligned-ai-for-inclusive-patient-matching-across-literacy-and-resource-settings/) [R].

### 1.2 Criteria parsing to computable form (USDM, CQL, OMOP, FHIR)

Nothing peer-reviewed yet evaluates LLM → USDM or LLM → CQL for eligibility. What exists:

- **OMOP SQL (JMIR Med Inform 2025):** GPT-4 concept mapping 48.5 % vs USAGI 32.0 %; llama3:8b highest effective-SQL rate (75.8 % vs GPT-4 45.3 %) because it hallucinated less (21.1 % vs 33.7 %); cohort Jaccard 0.81 (T1DM) but 0.00 (pregnancy) and 0.03 (T2DM); authors recommend hybrid LLM + rules (https://medinform.jmir.org/2025/1/e71252) [V]. **EC2Seq2Sql** (PLoS One 2026): BART parser + LLM agent; SQL exact-match 0.84, execution 0.91, clinical-match 0.88 on a Zhongshan Hospital HCC cohort (https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0341827) [V]. **C2Q 3.0** concept F1 0.891 with 34.5 % of SQL errors being logic errors (https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jbi.2024.104649) [V].
- **FHIR as target.** CHIP 2025 Shared Task 3 (Chinese NLP community): 51 criteria across 19 categories and 51 case reports; participants must emit FHIR Bundles plus retrieval logic; the overview concludes LLMs can produce standards-compliant code but need "explicit semantic layers" for safety (https://scholar.hit.edu.cn/en/publications/overview-ofmedical-nlp-code-generation-withfhir-forclinical-trial/; task page http://cips-chip.org.cn/2025/eval3, 503 at fetch time) [V/R]. FHIR Workbench (JMIR 2025): GPT-4o scores 0.999 on resource-ID tasks but note-to-FHIR conversion maxes at 0.363 (GPT-4.5-preview) (https://www.jmir.org/2025/1/e73540) [V]. HL7 adapted **EvidenceVariable** for structured eligibility (https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10582208) and Elimu showed molecular criteria are expressible in **CQL** (https://amia.secure-platform.com/symposium/gallery/rounds/82001/details/10246) [V].
- **USDM/M11.** CDISC intends USDM R3/4 to cover the full CeSHarP template; EligibilityCriterion/EligibilityCriterionItem map to SDTM TI; HL7 Vulcan's protocol IGs carry only partially structured content (https://www.appliedclinicaltrialsonline.com/view/navigating-toward-a-digital-clinical-trial-protocol; https://build.fhir.org/ig/HL7/vulcan-udp-ig/en/; https://hl7.org/fhir/uv/clinical-study-protocol/2026May/en/best-practice.html) [R]. A June 2026 FDA technical guide expects Trial Design Model datasets with SDTM submissions (https://intuitionlabs.ai/articles/cdisc-usdm-4-protocol-to-sdtm-guide) [R]. Faro won the CDISC 2025 AI Innovation Challenge for building USDM-centric libraries from legacy protocols (https://www.accessnewswire.com/newsroom/en/biotechnology/faro-wins-cdisc-2025-ai-innovation-challenge-for-protocol-library-solution-1078906) [R]. OHDSI CohortGenerator 1.1.0 (March 2026) still compiles CIRCE JSON, which is the natural compile target for a validated atom set (https://cran.r-universe.dev/CohortGenerator/doc/GeneratingCohorts.html) [V].

Implication: the two-layer criteria model in the spec is right, but the compile target should be CIRCE JSON (for OMOP) **and** FHIR EvidenceVariable/CQL (for TW Core FHIR), with the LLM never writing SQL.

### 1.3 Cascades and hybrid rules + LLM

No published study yet isolates a structured-filter → small model → large model cascade for screening. Closest evidence: UTSW's Tier-1 gate (and its documented false-negative risk) (https://www.medrxiv.org/content/10.64898/2026.03.20.26348890v1.full) [V]; **TrialTriage** (JMIR Form Res 2026), LLM extraction plus a deterministic 7-criterion engine with "Ambiguous → email the investigator → reclassify," on 90 synthetic cases (https://doi.org/10.2196/100779) [V]; Kindai's three-arm rule/LLM/hybrid design [V, pending]; iTEST (Taiwan) clinician-editable rules + NLP, accuracy 0.91 → 1.00 (https://doi.org/10.2196/80072) [V]. Cost anchors for the LLM tier: $0.02–0.11 (RECTIFIER), $0.12 (UTSW), $0.15–0.27 (Beattie), $0.17 vs $6.18 (OncoLLM vs GPT-4) (https://arxiv.org/pdf/2509.19327) [V]. The distribution-free cascade machinery (Learn-then-Test, conformal cascades, Trust-or-Escalate) is in `v3_topics.md` and still has no clinical-trial application in print (https://arxiv.org/abs/2110.01052v4; https://people.csail.mit.edu/tals/publication/relaxed_cascaded_conformal_prediction; https://arxiv.org/pdf/2407.18370) [V].

### 1.4 Calibration, abstention and selective prediction

- "Knowing When to Abstain" (arXiv 2601.12471, Jan 2026): conformal prediction over option logits at 90 % coverage; high-accuracy medical LLMs often fail to abstain; an explicit abstain option helps more than scale or prompting [V, preprint].
- Stability-aware prompt optimisation for clinical abstraction (arXiv 2601.22373): abstains when the conformal set size exceeds one or confidence falls below threshold; reports coverage–accuracy curves and ECE [V, preprint].
- Uncertainty-aware clinical text classification (arXiv 2509.19375): entropy-based abstention "discards about as many correct predictions as errors," i.e. does not reliably improve selective prediction [V, preprint].
- Token probabilities predict correctness better than verbalised confidence (AUROC 0.71–0.87 vs 0.52–0.68), including on MedQA-Taiwan (https://doi.org/10.2196/64348) [V].
- Geometry-calibrated conformal abstention (arXiv 2604.27914) and adaptive conformal semantic entropy (arXiv 2605.04295) give finite-sample guarantees for free-text answers [R].
- No paper applies conformal abstention to eligibility criteria. The first product to ship a per-criterion abstention rate with a stated coverage guarantee will be first.

### 1.5 Multilingual and code-mixed clinical NLP (Chinese, Japanese)

**Taiwan.** Code-mixed de-identification corpus of 1,700 discharge summaries: code-mixed training data is essential; zero-shot LLMs feasible but weaker (https://www.jmir.org/2024/1/e48443/) [V]. E-Da cardiac CT reports: 96.7 % mixed text; rules reach 99.98 % on numeric extraction (https://doi.org/10.1007/s10278-026-02009-8) [V]. CMUH nursing: fine-tuned Whisper cut mixed error rate 44.79 % → 6.67 %; LLM DART notes macro-F1 0.82, hallucination 2.51 %; monthly notes 32,724 → 65,417 in deployment (https://doi.org/10.2196/88567) [V]. NTUH psychiatry: LoRA Mistral event-extraction F1 0.925 on 500 summaries (https://doi.org/10.2196/94454) [V]. Multi-institution ICD-10-CM coding with BioMistral in a 13-week RCT of 10 coders (https://doi.org/10.1038/s41746-026-02541-5) [V]. FEMH pre-anaesthesia tool uses NHI cloud data as a second structured source (https://doi.org/10.2196/83893) [V]. EMPEC: medical-specialised LLMs underperform general ones on Traditional Chinese licensing exams (https://arxiv.org/html/2406.11328v1) [V]. Still **no Traditional Chinese clinical-note eligibility benchmark** exists.

**Japan.** Gemma-2-27B QLoRA macro-F1 0.906 ≈ Claude 3.5 Sonnet few-shot 0.875 on 773 primary-care notes (https://formative.jmir.org/2026/1/e84974) [V]; LLaMA-3.1-8B QLoRA only 0.49/0.59 micro-F1 on 73-label SOAP structuring (https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-026-58622-8) [V]; fine-tuned LLMs more robust than baselines for disease-name recognition on unseen progress notes (https://medinform.jmir.org/2025/1/e76773) [V]; JAMDAS EMR database used an LLM to extract exacerbations/hospitalisations for 32,258 asthma patients, with only 1.8–3 % having ACT scores recorded, a data-availability warning for feasibility (https://doi.org/10.1080/02770903.2026.2730888) [V]; Flatiron reports 100 % sensitivity/precision for ECOG PS extraction from Japanese notes (JSMO 2026 abstract) (https://resources.flatiron.com/publications/japanese-language-ehr-llm-extraction-of-longitudinal-unstructured-ecog-performance-status) [Vendor]; Tohoku University Hospital trained a medical LLM for discharge-summary generation (Gan To Kagaku Ryoho 2026) [V]; **J-ClinicalBench** (LREC 2026) covers nine Japanese clinical tasks [R]; JMedBench (https://www.arxiv.org/pdf/2409.13317) [V].

**China.** RAG improves ADE extraction from 18,432 Chinese notes with DeepSeek-V3, ERNIE 3.5 and GPT-4o (JMIR 2026, PMID 42574561) [V]; CMQOE zero-shot prompting on hypertension records (https://doi.org/10.1007/s11517-026-03527-x) [V]; ChatGLM eCRF: free-text accuracy 77.1 % vs 99.6 % manual (https://doi.org/10.1093/ehjdh/ztae066) [V]; hepatopathy pre-screening precision 0.921 (https://doi.org/10.1186/s13040-025-00458-5) [V].

**Cross-lingual.** DRAGON (28 Dutch IE tasks): 14B open models competitive; **translating to English before inference consistently degraded performance** (https://arxiv.org/pdf/2507.20859v1) [V, preprint]. PCP-Bot multilingual evaluation (Mandarin/Spanish/Hindi role-play, 31 bilingual participants) documents degradation of an English-built clinical chatbot in other languages (https://www.medrxiv.org/content/10.64898/2026.05.03.26352241) [V, preprint]. LANG-2 ("never translate before reasoning") is supported.

### 1.6 Small open models and benchmarks

- **CLUE**: nine of twelve biomedical-specialised models score lower than their general counterparts (https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.04067v3; https://github.com/tio-ikim/clue) [V]. **MedHELM** (Nat Med 2026): 121 tasks, LLM-jury (https://doi.org/10.1038/s41591-025-04151-2) [V]. **EHRSHOT**: LLM embeddings (GTE-Qwen2-7B, LLM2Vec-Llama-3.1-8B) match or beat specialised models on 15 prediction tasks [R, venue unverified]. **TrialPanorama** (npj Digit Med 2026): an 8B model trained on it beats 70B generic models on all eight (non-matching) tasks (https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.16097) [V]. **TREC CT 2023**: 40 topics, eight disorders, questionnaire-style patients; participants found GPT-3.5 reranking helps but "comparable results may be attained with less complex retrieval systems that utilise BM25" (https://pages.nist.gov/trec-browser/trec32/trials/overview; https://trec.nist.gov/pubs/trec32/papers/UNIMIB_IKR3.C.pdf; https://trec.nist.gov/pubs/trec32/papers/CSIROmed.C.pdf) [V]. **n2c2 2018**: see §0.8.
- **Structured-output reliability of 7–8B local models**: without schema enforcement, Mistral-7B had 18.6–21.4 % persistent parse failures; with grammar-constrained decoding, 70/70 valid in every run; prompt placement alone changed invalid outputs 1.7 % → 7.9 % (https://doi.org/10.3390/healthcare14142150) [V]. Constrained decoding is mandatory, not optional.
- **Agent benchmarks**: MedAgentBench best 69.7 % (https://doi.org/10.1056/AIdbp2500144); **FHIR-AgentBench** (ML4H 2026): 2,931 questions on MIMIC-IV-FHIR (https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.19319; https://proceedings.mlr.press/v297/lee26a.html); **FHIR-AgentEval**: 43 tasks with a resettable FHIR server and deterministic state validation (https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12919212/) [V]. None covers coordinator tasks.

### 1.7 LLMs for eCRF abstraction / EHR-to-EDC

- Direct EHR→EDC integration (AMIA Jt Summits 2026, PMID 42317828): manual transcription error rate **8.23 % (95 % CI 7.79–8.70) vs 0 % (0.00–0.03)** in a multi-study, multi-site assessment [V]. REDCap CDIS multisite (JBI 2026): 15–26 h setup per site; 764 persistent errors avoidable in 4,404 fields; 51 min saved per participant (https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jbi.2026.105060) [V]. Embedded EHR→EDC tool in phase I oncology (ESMO RWD 2025): 11,342 data points, 955 CRFs, **37 s per CRF**, 89 % labs (https://doi.org/10.1016/j.esmorw.2025.100112) [V]. SWOG cloud EHR→EDC (JSCDM 2025; https://doi.org/10.47912/jscdm.371) [V]. Vendor hour-savings (UCLH 67 h/patient; UCSF 208 h/52 patients) are from OpenClinica (https://www.openclinica.com/blog/lessons-from-the-field-get-ready-for-esource/) [Vendor].
- LLM abstraction: Stanford breast-oncology pipeline, 99 % recurrence / 96 % HER2 / 91 % stage concordance, 100 patients, retrospective (https://www.medrxiv.org/content/10.64898/2026.03.23.26349012) [V, preprint]; "Digital Registrar" local-LLM pathology abstraction across 10 cancer types, 893 internal + 242 TCGA reports (https://www.medrxiv.org/content/10.1101/2025.10.21.25338475.full.pdf) [R, fetch blocked]; Tempus NSCLC: LLM produced values for 90 % of elements with agreement ≥0.81 vs two abstractors on 222 cases (https://www.tempus.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/05/Manghnani_LLM_NSCLC_ASCO-2025_template.pdf) [Vendor]; Flatiron **VALID** framework (JCO CCI, April 2026): metrics vs expert abstraction, logic checks, replication of known findings (https://resources.flatiron.com/press/flatiron-health-publishes-first-peer-reviewed-validation-framework-for-ai-extracted-real-world-oncology-data-in-journal-of-clinical-oncology) [R]. No prospective eCRF-level LLM validation exists.

### 1.8 Enrollment forecasting

Deep Poisson-Gamma (arXiv 2507.23607): LM features from trial documents + Gamma uncertainty layer; trial-duration MAE **7.52 months**, ~3 months better than filter-and-fit; six-month coverage roughly doubled [V, preprint]. Bieganek AUC 0.78 (https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0263193); TrialEnroll PR-AUC 0.70; site-risk ML 98.9 % internal → 81.2 % external (https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ijmedinf.2026.106314); PI forecasts Brier 0.38 (https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0262862) [V]. A 2026 empirical-bioethics study found consent-document clarity does **not** predict accrual failure; start year and enrollment capacity do (https://doi.org/10.1007/s11673-026-10588-y) [V]. Vendor claims (Kumo "60 days earlier", Axtria) are unverified (https://kumo.ai/solutions/industries/healthcare/clinical-trial-enrollment/) [Vendor]. Nobody publishes a prospectively calibrated site-level forecast.

### 1.10 Agentic systems in clinical research

Peer-reviewed: Manus/OpenManus-style agents give modest gains for >10× tokens and >2× latency (https://doi.org/10.1038/s41746-026-02443-6) [V]; irAE-detection agent for trial staff, RCT crossover −40 % time (https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.02.26.26347179) [V, preprint]; Data-reflector restricts the LLM to intent and routes all computation through deterministic local functions to prevent hallucination (JAMIA Open 2026, PMID 42825291) [V]; a 31-component local multi-agent system on a 27B open model outperformed a frontier model with one generic prompt on manuscript audit (IJMI 2026, PMID 42574979) [V]; ProMem-Agent procedural memory on MIMIC-IV (IJMI 2026, PMID 42784988) [V]. Vendor: ConcertAI ACT (10–20 months saved) (https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20260202475918/en/ConcertAI-Launches-Accelerated-Clinical-Trials-Leveraging-Agentic-AI-to-Radically-Shorten-Trial-Timelines), Saama modular agents (https://www.saama.com/news/saama-introduces-modular-and-adaptive-ai-agents-for-clinical-innovation/), Medable CRA agents (https://www.clinicalleader.com/doc/agentic-ai-is-reshaping-clinical-monitoring-and-the-cra-role-again-0001) [Vendor]. Pattern that holds across the real studies: deterministic computation, typed tools, skills/procedural memory, and local models orchestrated well can beat a frontier model prompted once.

### 1.11 Failure modes

- **Fabricated or unsupported evidence**: >146,932 hallucinated references estimated in 2025 preprints (https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.07723); LLMs attach verbatim clinical citations to most claims but "frequently fail to fully substantiate them" (https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.15964); fidelity/applicability failures dominate over pure fabrication in grounded tools; a three-model judge panel showed negative inter-rater reliability on "verifiable" (https://arxiv.org/pdf/2604.17114) [V, preprints]. Conclusion: string-verified quotes, not LLM judges, must be the verifier.
- **Temporal errors**: CliniCIRCA — an LLM judge missed 169/175 (96.6 %) over-specified temporal tags (https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.19585); TIMER shows evaluating only record ends misses intermediate events (https://arxiv.org/pdf/2503.04176); EHR-ReasonCon targets note–table temporal inconsistencies on MIMIC-III (https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.26463); UTSW reports persistent "timing errors" in production [V]. No study compares long-context vs retrieval under an index-date look-back; TrialBox should.
- **Bias**: Soffer et al. (JAMIA 2026): 58 protocols, 5.3 M evaluations; eligibility stable across identities except homelessness; large shifts in inferential domains (adherence −0.595, resources −0.715) (https://doi.org/10.1093/jamia/ocag058) [V].
- **Plausible wrong rationales**: 75 % of incorrect decisions judged coherent (https://doi.org/10.1056/AIcs2400360) [V].
- **Prompt injection** via externally originated chart content (PMID 42745101) [V].
- **Reporting**: BMJ 2026 systematic review finds LLM-intervention trials incompletely reported (https://doi.org/10.1136/bmj-2026-100855) [V].

## 4. Fifteen innovation ideas grounded in the literature that no competitor ships

1. **Per-trial recall certificate with a blinded elusion sample.** No vendor or paper reports a certified lower bound on missed patients (UTSW and TrialGPT 2.0 both omit false negatives). Clopper–Pearson/QBCB machinery is in `v3_topics.md`; shipping it as a signed artefact is novel.
2. **Conformal abstention per criterion type.** Arxiv 2601.12471 and 2601.22373 give the recipe; no trial product publishes coverage guarantees. Report "abstained criteria" rate and route them to coordinators.
3. **Verifier-by-construction evidence**: every "met/not met" carries a verbatim span checked by string match and date window; LLM judges are shown to be unreliable verifiers (arXiv 2604.17114). Reject unverifiable outputs rather than scoring them.
4. **Index-date-aware retrieval with temporal unit tests.** UTSW's "timing errors" and CliniCIRCA's 96.6 % judge miss rate argue for a deterministic temporal checker and a regression set built from TIMER-style intermediate events.
5. **Prompt-injection quarantine for external documents**: referral letters, scanned reports and patient messages are tagged as untrusted provenance and never allowed to alter criteria state (PMID 42745101).
6. **Hybrid compile target: CIRCE JSON + FHIR EvidenceVariable/CQL from one validated atom set**, so the same criteria run on OMOP (TMUCRD-style) and TW Core FHIR; LLM never emits SQL (JMIR 2025 hallucination data).
7. **Local MedCPT/bge-m3 criterion-conditioned retrieval over long records**, following arXiv 2604.05190 and the myeloma agent result, with a published ablation against full-context input.
8. **NHI-cloud and NHI-code phenotype signals as computability tier 0** (FEMH already uses NHI cloud data clinically); no competitor models reimbursement-gated eligibility.
9. **Feasibility funnel with a data-availability disclosure per criterion**, motivated by JAMDAS (only 1.8–3 % of asthma patients had ACT scores) and CTSA findings; sponsors see "unknown" rates, not inflated counts.
10. **Prospectively calibrated enrollment forecasts** stored with every feasibility answer (Deep Poisson-Gamma-style intervals), producing the first site-level calibration dataset.
11. **Screen-fail reason capture that closes the 70 % "unrealised match" gap** (Surgery 2026; Yale 14 % of eligible enrolled): structured reasons feed criterion-impact simulation (Trial Pathfinder) back to sponsors.
12. **Grammar-constrained, schema-enforced outputs on every model call**, with pre-deployment proxy screening (Healthcare 2026), published as part of the validation pack.
13. **Code-mixed evaluation set and drift monitor** (script-share ratio, abbreviation rate), since translating before inference degrades extraction (DRAGON) and no Traditional-Chinese eligibility benchmark exists; publish it.
14. **Machine-to-machine feasibility answers** (MCP/FHIR) for sponsor agents (Medidata-Claude, Veeva AI, IQVIA.ai) and the ChatGPT-in-Epic plugin pattern, so the hospital answers questionnaires once from audited data.
15. **Model-portfolio with a licence/origin manifest and non-PRC tier**, validated per site with the recall certificate, enabling the same appliance to run tsuzumi-class Japanese models for the Kindai/NTT pattern.
