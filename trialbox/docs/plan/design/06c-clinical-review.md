## 0. Summary and overall score

**Overall clinical score: 3.4 / 5** (mean of the ten criteria in §1; not rounded up). The target of 4.5 is **not reached**, and §3.3 explains why it cannot be reached on paper alone.

What is genuinely good, and new since TB-REV-006b:

- TB-DS-17 is the first document in the set written from the clinical side in. Its "one rule" (no net work for any clinical role), the per-persona input tables with a reason per field, the interaction budget (§2.2), the one-click defaults (§2.3), the fixed role homes (§3), the full study cycle for trials *and* registries (§4), the alert budget with batching, quiet hours and snooze (§5.4), the personal-database import path (§6.2), the honest Medplum role statement (§7) and the pilot measurement plan with SUS, NASA-TLX and click instrumentation (§9) are the right shape and the right content. Few vendors write any of this down.
- The clinical-governance decisions are now right on paper: the investigator approves the ruleset (BD-13), only the investigator dismisses an AE suggestion (BD-08), the primary action on a negative verdict is "Send to PI" (P2), chart numbers appear only after an audited reveal (BD-04), mail carries pseudonyms only, sponsors see aggregates only.
- The repository already shows a PI weekly list with one-tap marks, a reason pick-list, an audited reveal and a CRC "My studies" home with a next action per study. The built list sorts by next visit date and gives a deterministic "why this patient fits" sentence. That is a credible seed for S5/S16.

Why it is 3.4 and not 4.5:

1. **The clinical layer exists only in two documents.** The 35 requirement rows that TB-DS-01 v0.3 added for it (FR-NOTIF, FR-STUDY, FR-UI-14..16, FR-REP-06..08, FR-FEAS-11/12, FR-ING-19..22, NFR-USAB-06..10) appear **zero times** in the architecture (TB-DS-02), the verification matrix (TB-DS-04), the interface control document (TB-DS-08) and the project plan (TB-PLAN-01). Nothing in the plan's 517 hours builds a PI home, a Monday mail per PI, a CRC digest, notification settings, a one-page approval screen, a reconciliation engine or a personal-database import with consent metadata. The plan is already at 1.6 × capacity. (C-02)
2. **"Shadow mode" and "no added load" contradict each other for the pilot.** Staff must keep screening by hand (ethics pack, TB-DS-00 §2) *and* mark candidates and record outcomes in TrialBox *and* review 100 charts per study. Net clinical time in the pilot is positive by construction, so NFR-USAB-06 and N-17 cannot be met during the pilot as designed. The documents do not say this. (C-01, C-04)
3. **Four load-bearing pieces of the CRC flow are unspecified**: how a TrialBox pseudonym is linked to the study's screening number and REDCap record in M1 without the CRC typing it; how the four contact outcomes map onto the seven registry statuses; where a study is set up (type, consent scope, ICF approval record, REDCap project link, team scope); and how the PI's in-clinic "Approach" reaches the CRC the same day. (C-06, C-07, C-14)
4. **The consent-explainer process is again written three ways** (BD-15 automatic release vs the BD-07 manual release still described in TB-TMPL-01 §6, TB-DS-11 H-11, TB-DS-02 §6.7 and TB-DS-15 OQ-06), and the automatic path has no step for the most common real-world event: the committee approves the ICF but asks for wording changes to the explainer. (C-05)
5. **The pilot measurement that everything rests on (the N-17 baseline) has no work package, no instrument, no owner hours and no date.** (C-03)

None of these is hard to write down. But three of the ten criteria (workload realism, site practice fit, measurability) can only move above 4 with input from the sites themselves: a time-motion baseline, interviews with CRCs and the research office, the hospitals' phone and REDCap policies, and a formative usability test of the new screens. §3 separates what a document pass can fix (estimated 4–5 working days) from what only the sites can settle.

### 3.3 Is 4.5 reachable on paper alone?

**No.** With the document pass in §3.1 the clinical score can honestly reach about **4.0–4.2**. Three criteria are capped below 4.5 by facts no document can supply:

- **Criterion 1 (workload realism)** cannot exceed about 3.5 until a baseline exists and at least one site has run Phase 2 (R-01, R-10). Every minute in TB-DS-17 §2.2 is (E), and the document says so itself.
- **Criterion 9 (site practice fit)** cannot exceed about 4.0 until the interviews (R-02, R-08, R-09) confirm or correct the personas, the device policy (R-04) and the committee's handling of explainers (R-07).
- **Criterion 10 (measurability and buildability)** cannot exceed about 4.0 until the baseline is collected and the clinical layer has passed a formative usability test (R-03), because "measurable" means the instrument has been used once.

A fair expectation: **≈ 4.0–4.2 after the document pass (about one week)**; **≈ 4.3–4.5 after R-02, R-03, R-04, R-05 and R-07 are in hand (late January 2027)**; **≥ 4.5 only after the pilot Phase-2 measurement (about May 2027)**, and only if the measurement comes out as designed. Any clinical review that gives 4.5 before a CRC and a PI at each hospital have used the screens and the baseline has been measured would be scoring the authors' intentions, not the design.

## Annex: method

- Scores are per criterion against what a site would do with the document as written; the overall score is the unweighted mean. 4.0 means "a site would adopt after minor clarifications", 3.0 "a site would send it back with practical questions", consistent with the scale used in TB-REV-006 and 006b.
