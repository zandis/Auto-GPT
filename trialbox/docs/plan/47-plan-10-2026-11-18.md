## 1. For the owner

### 1.1 The product, as you framed it

TrialBox is **a patient-finding engine with services around it**. One engine answers "does this patient meet these
criteria, as far as the record can say?" for coded data and notes alike, with "unknown never excludes" as its first
rule [15, read]. Every use (a sponsor's multicentre trial, a single-centre trial, an investigator-initiated study, a
registry, a post-marketing survey, later reimbursement) is a service that reads the same answers: feasibility counts,
the weekly list, a watch-list with the date a patient will qualify, one patient against every open study, alerts on a
new result, co-enrolment checks, exports [15, opinion]. The engine and its services are **shaped per site** (the
hospital's codes, documents and habits are data the steward fills, not code) [12, opinion], **per country** (a Japan
profile: law, committees, roles, words, forms, calendar) [11, opinion] and **per tier** (what each machine can do is
one record the whole product reads) [13, opinion]. It is **multicentre by construction**: a coordinating centre signs
a study package once; each hospital's box imports it, adapts only listed local fields and sends back only signed counts
[14, opinion]. No patient data ever leaves a hospital. It arrives **as a prepared machine** that a small hospital IT
team finishes in a Japanese browser wizard, with no file to edit and no command to type, and one page shows what the
site has and what it does not, and why [18, opinion; decisions 96, 101].

### 1.5 What you must supply or decide next: the ten that block the most

Your rulings cleared the first ten. These are the next, ordered by how much work waits on each [opinion]:

1. **Is the EDC module a condition of release 1.0?** (decision 40, sharpened). With one milestone, the release
   statement, the bundle and criteria 26 to 31 wait on it. Recommendation: no; it enters when two consecutive clean
   reviews say so, and the site uses its REDCap meanwhile.
2. **The accuracy bound and the coverage floor** (decisions 17 and 91). The gold set's size, the validation runs and
   criterion 1 wait on it. Recommendation: at 1.0, at most 5 % of eligible patients wrongly excluded per language
   (95 % upper bound, at least 124 eligible patients, two misses allowed), with at least 25 % of note lines decided on
   the frozen Japanese set; 2 % per language in 1.1 as the gold grows.
3. **Who writes the Japanese gold** (decision 19). The bound cannot be measured without it, and it is the longest
   human task. Recommendation: paid Japanese clinician writers on synthetic persons first, the site's de-identified
   sample after its ethics review.
4. **The delivered form** (decision 96). The site seed, the vendor preparation line and the hospital's purchase wait
   on it. Recommendation: a vendor-prepared machine from a maker with Japanese on-site warranty.
5. **The card** (decision 32, your question "5090?"). Recommendation: not a 5090; the RTX PRO 6000 Max-Q (section
   8.1). The order, the 300 W run and the preparation line wait on it.
6. **Who administers the box day to day** (decision 97). The Japanese operator's page and the key ceremony roles wait
   on it. Recommendation: the hospital's IT department, with the research office's person as data steward.
7. **The Japanese support partner** (decision 69). The on-site day, the first month and the spare wait on it.
   Recommendation: yes, before the box ships.
8. **"Complete" is what the capability page shows** (decision 101). The tracking you asked for waits on it.
   Recommendation: yes; a capability in the release is validated here or not available because of the tier or your
   ruling, never "not built yet".
9. **Model builds under non-Apache terms** (decision 93). The bake-off's candidate list waits on it. Recommendation:
   only Apache-2.0 or MIT builds in the shipped box; a "community" licence only in development runs.
10. **The bake-off spend and how the cap is counted** (decision 95). Stage H and stage G wait on it. Recommendation:
