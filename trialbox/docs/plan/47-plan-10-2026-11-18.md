## 1. For the owner

### 1.1 The product, as you framed it

TrialBox is **a patient-finding engine with services around it**. One engine answers "does this patient meet these
criteria, as far as the record can say?" for coded data and notes alike, with "unknown never excludes" as its first
rule [15, read]. Every use (a sponsor's multicentre trial, a single-centre trial, an investigator-initiated study, a
registry, a post-marketing survey, later reimbursement) is a service that reads the same answers: feasibility counts,
the weekly list, a watch-list with the date a patient will qualify, one patient against every open study, alerts on a
new result, co-enrolment checks, exports [15, opinion]. The engine and its services are **shaped per site** (the
hospital's codes, documents and habits are data the steward fills, not code) [12, opinion], **per country** (a Japan
profile: law, committees, roles, words, forms, calendar) [11, opinion] and **per tier** (what each machine can do is
one record the whole product reads) [13, opinion]. It is **multicentre by construction**: a coordinating centre signs
a study package once; each hospital's box imports it, adapts only listed local fields and sends back only signed counts
[14, opinion]. No patient data ever leaves a hospital.

### 1.3 What must be true before the first site goes live

Ordered by what waits for what: the data first, then what reads it, then what the site and its people must do
(section 5.1 has the measurement for each):

1. **The SS-MIX2 reader reads the specification**: the visit key, the diagnosis fields, one dose per administration,
   the extended-storage layout; and its tests are written from the specification, not from TrialBox's own exporter
   [12; coordinator].
2. **The privacy guard masks Japanese identifiers**: katakana names, old-form kanji names, insurance numbers [11].
3. **The two engine defects are closed**: a negated inclusion never runs as its opposite; a mixed inclusion is never
   met on its age part alone [15; coordinator].
4. **The legal footing is written**: the purpose-of-use line the hospital posts, the 2026 opt-out template, the access
   rule (only the study's delegation-log staff see a pre-consent list), and a Japanese regulatory reader's note [11].
5. **The documents the hospital's IT department asks for** exist in Japanese, the containers are hardened, the audit
   head is signed hourly, and the outside penetration test has no open High finding [16].
6. **The site questionnaire is answered** (review 12 section 9: the system, versions, SS-MIX2 option, local masters,
   REDCap) and the site profile is filled from it [12].
7. **The tier is chosen and measured**: the RTX PRO 6000 Max-Q is recommended; its 300 W run, the serving runs and the
   capacity statement for a hospital of about 560 beds are filed [13, 08].
8. **The Japanese wording is read by clinicians** (you ruled yes): the core terms and the patient-facing texts [11, 09].
9. **Validation is executed at the site**: installation and operational qualification on the delivered box, the
   hospital's own counts reconciled, and the first study run in shadow beside the coordinator's manual pre-screening
   [07, 12, 15].

Meanwhile the first site runs **its own REDCap through TrialBox's REDCap link** (review 12 covers REDCap at the site).
The EDC module enters only when its release criterion is met [coordinator].
