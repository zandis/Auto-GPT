# Plan 8: the plan to finish (revision 2 of 2026-11-17, lane R)

**Reader:** the owner and the integrator. A draft: the integrator adopts or amends it; the owner rules on section 9.

**Why there is a revision 2.** Revision 1 drew its finish line at "a hospital pilot may start" and left work for
"during the pilot". The owner rejected that framing on 2026-11-17, and he is right: a hospital is a customer, and a
product is finished and verified before it goes to a customer. In his words: do our best to prepare and finish all
of it, then move on to the hospital, not the other way round; the plan of building and of verification is the key.
This revision is written on that basis. It has no "during the pilot" band.

**What it rests on.** Two reviews of master `8f5d429c` (train 66), both of 2026-11-17:
`docs/reviews/2026-11-17-journey-and-simplicity.md` (its cards are cited as **R1 to R58**) and
`docs/reviews/2026-11-17-full-review.md` (cards **F1 to F45** and **G1 to G10**); and the work list of
`42-plan-2026-11-16.md` section 4.

**What it keeps.** Every owner's ruling: the rules of `38-handover-2026-11-15.md`; the answers of plan v7.3
(`33-plan-v7.md` section 2); D-503 to D-519; `docs/decisions/OWNER-RULINGS-2026-11-16.md` (D-1020 to D-1039);
D-1060 to D-1064. **No dates** (D-1027): the order is by what depends on what. One date is a fact of the calendar,
not a build date: the vulnerability waivers lapse on 2026-12-10. Where this plan thinks a ruling should change, or
an item should be left out of release 1.0, it does not plan against the ruling and it does not cut the item: it
asks, in sections 6 and 9.

## 1. One page

**The finish line is release 1.0:** a complete product, verified by us, ready to be implemented at a hospital.
What a hospital's team then does is implementation only: install it, load its own lists, connect its own systems,
and run its site acceptance. No development and no unfinished feature is planned for the site.

**Where the project is.** The product is broad and carefully built: it reads a hospital's records, compiles a
protocol's criteria, screens, lists candidates with their evidence, carries the investigator's marks to the
coordinator, keeps the site's logs from consent to study close, drafts values for the EDC under a signature, and
guards all of it with a role matrix, a signed audit chain, signed updates and encrypted backups. It runs end to end
on a synthetic hospital with a stub model, in three languages. Two things separate it from release 1.0:

- **It is not yet verified.** No real model pair has a passing qualification record. Neither installer has
  finished on real hardware. No restore has been timed. No figure the repository quotes was measured on what
  ships. No validation record has been executed.
- **It is not yet whole or simple.** Parts of the life cycle the owner asked for are still to be built (the
  former waves 6 to 11 of plan v7.3). And what is built grew by adding: a coordinator has 4 menu items and 25 under
  "More"; the guide on the home page ends at "Enrolled"; a candidate and a participant are two unlinked records;
  a site cannot switch a module off.

**The whole distance, counted** (section 8 has the table):

- **Build (band A):** 79 items: 17 small, 43 medium, 19 large. 11 of them are
  candidates the owner may choose to leave out of 1.0 (section 6); none is cut by this plan.
- **Verify (band B):** 20 verification activities, each with an entry condition, an exit condition and a record.
  Ten can start now.
- **Release and implementation kit (band C):** one bundle, one pack of records, one kit a hospital's team follows.
- **Release criteria:** twelve tests (section 3). Release 1.0 ships when all twelve pass.

**The shape of the path.**

```
 BUILD (A) ----------------------------------------------> feature-complete --> RELEASE CANDIDATE
   |  each item lands with its own tests, by train                                   |
 VERIFY (B)                                                                          v
   stage 0: can start now, on master        ---------------------------->  stage 3: on the release candidate
   stage 1: after the qualification record  ---------------------------->   every module's walk; first-time users;
   stage 2: after the signed bundle and the hardware ------------------->   the kit followed by a stranger; the pack
                                                                                     |
 RELEASE AND KIT (C)                                          release 1.0  <---------+
                                                                   |
 AT A HOSPITAL: implementation only: install, the site's lists and endpoints, accounts, site acceptance
```

Verification does not wait for the build to end. Most of it starts now and runs on master every night; only the
last stage waits for the release candidate. What a verification finds goes into the one findings register and is
built or fixed before release.

**In:** the whole trial life cycle, as the owner has asked for it.

- Everything in plan 42 section 4: the safety and go-live code (4a), the proof runs (4b), the former waves 6 to
  11 of plan v7.3 (4e), and the items of the outside review that the owner adopted (D-1037: the CI findings, the
  TFDA pre-fill rule checked, the independent check of the masker, shadow mode with its report, provenance on every
  AI output), with its "build later" list (4f).
- The cards of the two reviews: R1 to R58, F1 to F45, G1 to G10.
- The link to the owner's own EDC, as the record for CRF data (section 9, decision 3 confirms the form).

**Out, because the owner has declined it** (plan 42 section 4g; D-1037's declined list; D-1033; D-1036): an MCP
endpoint; a sponsor A2A interface; an ePRO app; an identity provider inside the box; prompt-optimisation and
RAG-scoring tools; a receiver for REDCap's Data Entry Trigger; a supervised remote session into a hospital's box;
a self-hosted CI runner while the repository is public; a separate phone app (the phone-first pages of module 0
take the place of T-733).

**Nothing else is out.** Where this plan thinks an item is a poor fit for 1.0, it is in band A all the same, marked
"candidate to leave out", and listed in section 6 with what is lost and what is saved, for the owner to rule.

**Evaluation (shadow) mode** is in the product as a feature: a site uses it for its own acceptance, to see how
the box and its own staff agree on its own patients. It is not the way we prove the product; band B is.

## 9. Decisions only the owner can make (seven)

**1. The release criteria.** Adopt the twelve criteria of section 3 as the test that release 1.0 may ship?
*Recommendation: yes.* The first six are the integrator's, sharpened; 7 to 9 make usability, erasure and evidence
testable; 10 to 12 are what a whole-life-cycle release needs.

**2. What a site may switch on at implementation, and what our release verifies.** Release 1.0 verifies **every
module** (criterion 10). At implementation a site switches on the modules it uses (Feasibility; Screening; Conduct;
the EDC link; Monitoring; Data stewardship), and may add the others later without a new release. Evaluation mode is
offered to every site as the first mode of Screening, for its own acceptance.
*Recommendation: yes.* It keeps your ruling that the REDCap send is live at go-live (plan v7.3, Q4): the send is
built and verified in 1.0; whether a given site switches the EDC link on is that site's choice.

**3. The EDC of record.** Is your own EDC the record for CRF data, with TrialBox's data pages a working aid for
the site's own log, and do values enter the EDC only through the signed Send?
*Recommendation: yes to both;* it settles the open item of plan 42 section 4e. Two things are then needed from
you, through the integrator, so that nobody reads the EDC's repository (D-1060): the EDC's page-address pattern for
a record, a visit and a form; and a test instance of the EDC for V14.

**4. What to leave out of 1.0.** Rule on the table of section 6, row by row.
*Recommendation:* leave out the other hardware forms, the opportunity feed and hub, use from outside the hospital,
the HL7 v2 engine, the FHIR-to-CDISC mapping, ICH M11 / USDM, and our own measurement aids; **keep in** the research
cards and the other four connectors only if you have a site that will use them in its first year. Each row left
out shortens both the build and the verification; none is lost, each goes to the release after.

**5. The means the verification needs from you.** (a) The reference workstation ordered (V10, V11, V17 and V20
wait for it). (b) The carve and the CGRD lists on your PC (V3, V6, V15). (c) REDCap 17 (V14). (d) Your three
testers for round 1 now, and new first-time people for round 3 (V13). (e) Three names: the security owner of the
waivers, which lapse on 2026-12-10; a second support person; and whether Aimedics keeps a loaner box.
*Recommendation:* (a) is the longest wait in the whole plan: order it now.

**6. Outside eyes, and review, for what is released.** (a) One outside penetration test on the release candidate
(a cost: the estimate comes to you first, by your rule 3). (b) A regulatory consultant's reading of the intended-use
paper and of the TFDA pre-fill interpretation (D-859), also a cost. (c) For the release only, one named human
reviews changes to the 18 safety-core files. (d) Formal validation records are generated for the release, not
hand-written.
*Recommendation: yes to all four.* (c) **would change D-311 for the release only**; it gates no development and no
merge, only what is shipped. Your rule of no development gates is kept.

**7. Two product rules.** (a) May the legacy pages leave the menu, the opening page be rebuilt around roles and
