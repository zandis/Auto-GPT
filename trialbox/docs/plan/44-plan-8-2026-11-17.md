## 1. One page: where the project is, what "finished" means, the shortest honest path

**Where it is.** The product is broad and carefully built. It reads a hospital's records, compiles a protocol's
criteria, screens, lists candidates with their evidence, carries marks to the coordinator, keeps the site's logs
from consent to study close, drafts values for the EDC under a signature, and guards all of it with a role matrix,
a signed audit chain, signed updates and encrypted backups. It runs end to end on synthetic data with a stub model,
in three languages, in about seven minutes.

**What it is not yet.** It is not yet *proven* and not yet *simple*.

- **Not proven.** No real model pair has a passing qualification record. Neither installer has finished on real
  hardware. No restore has been timed. No figure the repository quotes was measured on what ships. No validation
  record of any kind has been executed.
- **Not simple.** The console grew by adding. A coordinator has 4 menu items and 25 under "More"; the guide on the
  home page ends at "Enrolled"; a candidate and a participant are two unlinked records; a site cannot switch
  anything off; help understood 15 of 50 plain phrases.

**What "finished" means in this plan.** Not "every feature". **Finished = one hospital can start a pilot on real
data and not be misled**: a qualified model pair on a box installed and restored the way the site will do it; a
first user who can follow the journey without a guide at their shoulder; a patient's opt-out and a participant's
withdrawal that do what the screen says; and a validation pack the hospital's own group can read, generated from
what the repository already holds. Section 2 turns that into nine tests.

**The shortest honest path** has five moves, and the first can start today on every lane at once:

1. **Stop adding.** Freeze the paper (one plan, one findings register), the deployment forms and the menu
   (section 5). Fix the three things that mislead a user today.
2. **Prove what ships.** One chain, each link waiting for the one before: a qualification record on the shipped
   box → the confirming launch → a production box from the signed bundle, wiped and restored against a clock →
   the Windows install → one hospital day read, mapped and screened → one signed value into the EDC and back.
3. **Make the promises true.** Erasure in every store inside the box; withdrawal honoured; the audit event in the
   same transaction as its change; one way for a value to enter the EDC.
4. **Make it followable.** Link the journey end to end, prune the menu, give each role "what needs you today",
   put patients first on the phone, and let a site switch on only the modules it uses.
5. **Generate the evidence.** A release record, a traceability table with real results, IQ and OQ records from
   the box's own checks: produced by a command, not written by hand.

Moves 3, 4 and 5 do not wait for move 2. Only the pilot itself waits for all five.

## 7. Decisions only the owner can make (seven)

**1. The definition of done.** Adopt the nine criteria of section 2 as the test of "ready for a hospital pilot"?
*Recommendation: yes.* The first six are the integrator's, sharpened; the seventh is your own complaint made
testable; the eighth and ninth are what a hospital will ask before real data.

**2. What the pilot switches on.** Start the pilot with **Feasibility and Screening** on, in the mode where the
coordinator answers before seeing the box's answer (for the first study, so the pilot measures the person and the
box together); switch **Conduct** on for a study when its first patient consents; keep **CRF pre-fill** off until
its prompt is qualified?
*Recommendation: yes.* The first users then meet about a third of today's menu. **This would change a ruling:** in
plan v7.3 (Q4) you ruled the API send to REDCap live at go-live. The send stays built and tested (criterion 6); the
question is only whether it is switched on at the pilot's first day. If you keep Q4 as it is, cards R43 to R48 move
from "during" to "before".

**3. The EDC of record.** Is your own EDC the record for CRF data, with TrialBox's data pages a working aid for
the site's log, and do values enter the EDC only through the signed Send?
*Recommendation: yes to both.* It settles plan 42 section 4e's open item. If yes, please pass through the
integrator the EDC's page-address pattern for a record, a visit and a form, so "Open in the EDC" can be built
without anyone reading the EDC's repository (D-1060).

**4. Three names.** Who is the security owner of the vulnerability waivers (they lapse on 2026-12-10); who is the
second support person; and will Aimedics keep one loaner box per country?
*Recommendation:* name one person for the waivers today and let the dates be staggered; name a backup per language
and promise a one-hour incident answer in cover hours only; keep one shared loaner and state "five working days".

**5. Evidence and review for what goes to a hospital.** (a) Should formal validation records return at the first
real trial record of any kind, not only at pre-fill, and be generated rather than hand-written? (b) For the
pilot's release only, may one named human review changes to the 18 safety-core files? (c) Who signs the site's
validation summary?
*Recommendation:* (a) yes; (b) yes: **this would change D-311 for the pilot's release only**; it gates no
development, only what is shipped to a hospital; (c) the hospital's own validation group or trial office, with the
generated pack. Your rule of no development gates is kept: none of this stops a card from being built or merged.

**6. Simplify, and freeze the paper.** May the legacy pages leave the menu; may the opening page be rebuilt
around roles and study phases, replacing the rule "at most four items; everything else under More" (D-104); and
may the plan, review and decision folders be frozen as an archive, with one living plan and one findings register?
