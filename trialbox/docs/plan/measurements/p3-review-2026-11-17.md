# Launch P3 reviewed offline: why sigir ran slower, and the pathology report that moved, 2026-11-17 (card H-36)

**Reader:** the integrator, and the lanes that own what this page finds

This page reviews launch P3 against launch P2b. P3 is run `box-20261117T153147Z-15108f46`, on master `15108f46`,
2026-11-17 from 15:32 to 17:57 UTC; its stored sets are on branch `integrator/p3-artefacts`, folder
`trialbox/.p3-artefacts/`. P2b is run `box-20261117T073759Z-57263236`, on master `57263236`; its sets are on
`integrator/p2b-artefacts`, folder `trialbox/.p2b-artefacts/`. Neither branch is merged, and nothing from them is
copied here beyond the figures quoted. The data is public: SIGIR 2016 (NCBI TrialGPT), JMED-Personas (CC BY 4.0) and
TCGA-Reports (CC BY 4.0). It holds no hospital data.

The review used lane H's branch `claude/lane-h-36-p3-sigir-slower`, with no model, GPU or network. The commands
(`P2B` and `P3` are the two artefact folders):

```
python -m tools.public_accuracy.timing P2B/sigir.tgz P3/sigir.tgz --json timing-sigir.json
python -m tools.public_accuracy.replay P3/sigir.tgz --index-day 2026-11-18 --build-day 2026-11-17 \
    --json replay-b-sigir.json
python -m tools.public_accuracy.replay P3/jmed.tgz P3/tcga.tgz --index-day 2026-11-17 --build-day 2026-11-17 \
    --json replay-b-jt.json
python -m tools.public_accuracy.trace P3/sigir.tgz --ref 2026-11-17 --json trace-p3.json
```

`tools.public_accuracy.timing` is new (this card). It puts two stored runs of one set side by side, protocol by
protocol: each job's wall time, the model calls of each job by prompt and alias, schema repairs, cache hits, SCREEN's
tokens and pairs judged, the judged-pair store, the order the protocols were sent, the ones the budget cut, and how
many jobs of each type were in flight at once. It says where the time is, not why. The other figures are read from
the stored files named beside them (`work/` is inside each set's `.tgz`).

**No figure on this page meets a target.** A target is met only by a launch (D-1064 item 4). Each finding says whether
it is **shown** (read from the runs' records or the code) or a **hypothesis**.

## The answer

- **The extra time is in APPROVE, not in the model calls (shown).** Over the 66 sigir protocols both runs ran, the
  jobs' own wall time went from 10,379 s to 19,328 s. APPROVE accounts for all of it and more: 5,357 s to 14,442 s
  (+9,085 s). FEAS went down (4,537 s to 4,330 s) and SCREEN went up by 71 s (486 s to 557 s)
  (`timing-sigir.json`, `totals.wall_*`; each job's `metrics.wall_seconds` in `work/box-calls.log`). APPROVE makes no
  model call (`metrics.llm_calls` 0 on all 66 APPROVE jobs of both runs).
- **One approval costs about 41 s more, whatever the protocol (shown).** jmed and tcga run one protocol at a time. Their
  three APPROVE jobs took 30.4 s, 29.4 s and 29.7 s on P2b, and 71.6 s, 70.8 s and 71.3 s on P3, for protocols of 1 and
  2 criteria (`work/box-calls.log` of `jmed.tgz` and `tcga.tgz`). On sigir the extra does not grow with the protocol's
  size either: NCT00002897 (3 criteria) went from 81 s to 223 s, NCT00021320 (38 criteria) from 86 s to 208 s
  (`work/progress.json`, `draft`).
- **Three streams multiply it (shown in part).** sigir runs 3 protocols at once in both runs (`report.md`: `"streams":
  3`, `"in_flight_max": 3`). On P3, 2 or more APPROVE jobs were in flight in 78 % of the set's seconds (P2b: 50 %),
  and an approval took 219 s on average against 71 s alone. That approvals wait on each other in the compiler (a
  lock, or one worker) is a **hypothesis**: the records hold each job's start and wall time, not where it waited.
- **#996, #997 and #989 are not the cause (shown).** They act in FEAS (#996) and SCREEN (#997, #989), and those two
  together moved by −136 s over the 66 protocols. Model calls per protocol are the same in both runs: 556 FEAS calls
  on P2b, 538 on P3; 213 judge calls on P2b, 223 on P3. The judge prompt's size did not change (median 2,454 and
  2,468 characters), and `asking` is in no prompt.
- **Whose fix (hypothesis on the line, shown on the route).** The route is criteria-compiler's `/approve`
  (`Compiler.approve`, lane A2's service). Between the two commits it gained these fixed steps: `refresh_installed`
  (E-TERM-3, D-1048), `stamp_differs` (the TERM-2 follow-up), `site_list_blocks` (E-TERM-3) and one `ai.confirmed`
  per criterion (D-989 step 2b). Which of them costs the 41 s is not in any record. A card (lane A2, S) times each
  step, and the fix goes to the lane that owns that step. Lane H's driver changed nothing that explains it: the same
  streams, the same budget, the same order.
- **The pathology figure: one report moved (shown).** TCGA-EM-A3FK, a thyroid report, was excluded on P2b and left
  open on P3. No report moved the other way. 939 of P3's 1,020 note answers on tcga were read from the judged-pair
  store, not asked again. So P3's 378 is not a second independent measurement of P2b's 379.

## 2. Attribution

Each share is of the +8,950 s of job time the 66 protocols gained (sum of FEAS, APPROVE and SCREEN wall).

- **(a) More note questions and pairs: #996, and the 3 protocols #997 unblocked (shown; under 1 %).** P3's drafts
  flag 5 lines `qualifier_dropped` (#996). S3 judged 26 more pairs and the judge alias was asked 1 question fewer
  (`totals`). SCREEN as a whole grew by 71 s. The three protocols that failed on P2b, NCT00282269, NCT00703417 and
  NCT00974064, now ran SCREEN for 17 s, 6 s and 23 s (46 s of the 71). FEAS, where #996 acts, went down by 207 s.
- **(b) Larger prompts or slower calls: #989's `asking` (shown; none).** The judge prompt is the same size: median 2,454
  characters on P2b and 2,468 on P3, mean 2,510 and 2,506 (`work/raw-traces.json`, each `judge@5` trace's `prompt`). No
  prompt and no stored trace attribute holds `asking`: it is on the audit's `llm.call` record only. SCREEN tokens in per
  SCREEN model call are 599 on P2b and 572 on P3 (`screen_metrics.tokens_in` over `screen_metrics.llm_calls`, 185 and
  223; P2b's three failed SCREEN jobs recorded none). The judged-pair store read nothing on sigir in either run, so
  `asking` cannot have changed its hits. SCREEN took 2.3 s per traced judge call on P2b and 2.5 s on P3.
- **(c) Fewer cache hits, the judged-pair store (shown; none).** The store read 0 on sigir in both runs
  (`cascade.judged_store.read`). The gateway's cache hit 23 calls on P2b and 17 on P3, out of 769 and 761: 6 more
  calls asked, at about 2 s to 8 s each.
- **(d) Slower serving (shown in part; none seen).** The same model builds served both runs (`tb.build_sha` of the
  traces; `summary.json`, `provenance.weight_manifests`, the same three manifests). The traces keep each call's end
  time only, not its length or tokens, so tokens per second per call cannot be computed. Per call, FEAS took 8.2 s of
  job time on P2b and 8.0 s on P3 (FEAS wall over its `ir_extract` and `concept_map` calls), and SCREEN 2.3 s and
  2.5 s. P3's FEAS ran with fewer jobs in flight (0.63 against 1.07), which favours P3, so this is no proof that
  serving speed was equal.
- **(e) Concurrency (shown: the same setting; hypothesis: it multiplies APPROVE).** Both runs ran sigir with 3
  streams (`report.md`, sigir: `"streams": 3`, `"in_flight_max": 3`; jmed and tcga: 1). With one stream an approval
  costs 41 s more. On sigir the approvals overlap (section 1), and each takes about 3 times its time alone (219 s
  against 71 s). If approvals run one at a time in the compiler, P3's 66 approvals hold it for about 66 × 71 s =
  4,690 s, 68 % of the set's 6,851 s; P2b's 104 for 104 × 30 s = 3,120 s, 46 % of 6,723 s. That fits the shares of
  seconds with an approval in flight (93 % and 79 %), but it is a **hypothesis**.
- **(f) Everything else (shown).** The remaining +9,085 s is APPROVE: about 99 % of the gain, after (a)'s 71 s and
  FEAS's −207 s.

**What the records cannot separate.** No record holds a step's time inside a job. So the 41 s per approval cannot be
put on one line of `Compiler.approve` from the stored sets, and the waiting of approvals on each other cannot be told
from their own work.

## 3. Whose fix

**The route (shown).** The time is in criteria-compiler's `/approve`, run by the orchestrator's APPROVE scenario
(`orchestrator.scenarios.approve.run` calls `compiler.approve`; `criteria_compiler.service.Compiler.approve`). APPROVE
calls no model, so neither the gateway nor serving is in it. The orchestrator's APPROVE scenario did not change
between the commits (`git log 57263236..15108f46 -- services/orchestrator/scenarios/approve.py` is empty).

**The steps the route gained between `57263236` and `15108f46` (shown by `git diff`; which one costs the 41 s is a
hypothesis).** Each runs once per approval, whatever the protocol's size:
- `refresh_installed(self.d.term)`, first thing (E-TERM-3, D-1048): it reads every site list and TERM-2 table on the
  box again, and the NHI tables shipped in the image (H-17, D-1127) when the box loaded none. A compile does the
  same, and FEAS on one stream grew by 1 s to 4 s only (jmed 13.5 s and 8.3 s to 17.5 s and 11.8 s; tcga 8.5 s to
  9.5 s), so this step alone is not likely to be the 41 s.
- `stamp_differs(rs.manifest, self.d.term)` (the TERM-2 follow-up) and `site_list_blocks(rs.manifest, self.d.term)`
  (E-TERM-3): each reads the terminology's stamp or refusals again.
- one `ai.confirmed` audit record per criterion with model calls (D-989 step 2b, `tb_common.provenance.confirmed`).
  It is per criterion, and the extra does not grow with criteria, so it is not likely to be the 41 s either.

Nothing on lane H's driver line explains it: the same streams, the same budget (7,200 s), the same order. Serving
variance does not either: APPROVE calls no model.

**So:** the fix is in the APPROVE route. Lane A2 owns the route; the step that costs the time may be lane E's
(terminology). The first card is a measurement, not a guess:

- **Lane A2, size S:** time each step of `Compiler.approve` on one made-up protocol at `15108f46` and at
  `57263236`, on the box's image with its shipped tables (APPROVE needs no model), and record the steps' seconds in
  the APPROVE job's metrics so a launch's records show them. Then the fix, size S to M, in the lane that owns the
  step. Evidence: section 1 and the jmed and tcga APPROVE jobs.
- **Lane A2, size S, if the hypothesis of (e) holds:** let approvals of different rulesets run side by side in the
  compiler. Evidence: section 1, `in_flight`.

Until then a sigir launch with 3 streams needs about 104 s per protocol, 10,800 s for 104, against the 7,200 s budget.
Raising the budget hides the cause, and is the integrator's choice (a question below).
