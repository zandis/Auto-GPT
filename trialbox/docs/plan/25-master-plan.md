## 1. Scope and intended use

### 1.1 What goes live at both sites

Everything the owner listed, at both CGMH (Taiwan) and Makoto's site (Japan):

1. Whole-hospital screening on the site's feed, with the real model pair on the production gateway path.
2. Sub-PI delegation, the candidate view and referral through the treating physician, who may be unknown to the box.
3. The OMOP CDM v5.4 core in Postgres with the lake exported from it, provenance, conflicts and the vocabulary schema.
4. Study-patient profiles: the cited page, the twin, annotations, pgvector, "patients like me"; setpoints and the gap finder behind flags for PI and steward.
5. REDCap EDC prefill with the CRC confirming each value, under GAMP 5 validation executed inside the three months.
6. The steward role, the data-operations console and the statistics page.
7. The clinical item catalogue loaded as data with the four first specialty templates.
8. Opt-out register, IRB and ICF warnings, delegation-log copy with the monthly comparison, SAE acknowledgement and clocks, printed reminder card and calendar file, inputs for drug accountability, TMF and invoicing.
9. The platform fixes of waves 1 to 5 that a real-data pilot needs, MFA and AD/LDAP, Windows/WSL2 hardening, signed audit heads, scheduled off-box backups, release identity, SBOM.

"Build all, use all" means every item above is used with real patients at both sites in the pilot, not demonstrated on synthetic data. §4.4 says what happens to each item if its gate slips: nothing is cut quietly; a slip is an owner decision with the fallback named.

### 1.2 Intended-use and non-device statement (draft)

To appear in the console footer, every PDF, XLSX and mail, the 開示書, the IRB pack and sales material, in en, ja and zh-TW (the ja and zh-TW texts of TB-DS-16 §2 are kept and extended):

> TrialBox supports clinical research operations at a hospital. It builds a pseudonymised research repository from the hospital's own records, estimates how many patients may meet a research study's eligibility criteria, prepares lists of possible candidates with the record passages they rest on, lists the patients whose records do not match a criterion for the investigator's review, writes cited patient summaries for research staff, and drafts research-record values for a qualified person to confirm. TrialBox does not diagnose, treat or prevent disease, does not decide whether a patient is eligible for a study, does not recommend treatment, does not alert clinicians, and does not contact patients. Every output is reviewed by a qualified person. TrialBox is not a medical device (本ソフトウェアは医療機器ではありません / 本軟體非醫療器材，不作為診斷依據).

The product's own words follow the statement (independent review PR-12): the page built by D-143 as "Excluded patients and why" is renamed **"Criteria not matched"** (ja 基準に合致しない患者, zh-TW 未符合條件之病人) in every language, the API and the reports; a patient is "not matched on criterion X by record Y", never "excluded"; "除外", "排除" and "excluded" join the forbidden-word check (WS-13.7) everywhere except this plan's risk file, where the hazard is still named plainly. Counsel reads the statement against the live UI, not only against TB-DS-16 (WS-15.4).

Conditions that keep the statement true, written into the risk file and tested by the walk's forbidden-word check: no score or risk is shown as a clinical finding; personal lab setpoints are numbers with a "check" mark on screening values, never alerts; the gap finder is a study-screening rule shown to PI and steward under a research-use label and to a treating physician only inside a study referral, as numbers without a disease name, behind a switch that is off; no forecast layer; nothing writes to the EMR; nothing reaches a patient. A written PMDA/MHLW 該当性 view and a TFDA inquiry record are sought before any of this changes (WS-15.4).

### 1.3 Not in the pilot (dated, not dropped)

Randomisation (never in TrialBox); mail with answer buttons for the treating physician and doctor accounts (P1, WS-18); opt-in patient messages via hospital SMS or LINE (P1/P2, WS-18); the forecast layer (device route, P2); consortium exchange between the two sites beyond the registry and a two-box count test (WS-09 runs on synthetic sites in the pilot; real cross-border exports need the DSA and MOHW approval, P1); the service merge to five processes (P1); a second model pair for a non-PRC-model tier (only on a written request).

## 3. Work breakdown structure

Conventions. **Size** is in agent work-sessions (S): one session is one agent producing one reviewed pull request with tests, about one working day of agent effort; **R** is owner or reviewer hours (Changfu or Makoto) for review and decisions. **Owner** names who does the work: agent, Changfu, Makoto, Aimedics (a human on the vendor side: contracts, orders, insurance), site IT, site privacy office, IRB, counsel. **Trace** names the user requirement (§5), the risk (§6) and the test (§8) each item answers; the matrix `docs/plan/25-traceability.csv`, the §5 "Plan" cells and the totals below are **generated** from these tables by `tools/gen_traceability.py` (checked in CI and by `tests/unit/test_gen_traceability.py`), never typed twice. **Gate** is the quality gate of §4 the item must pass before; **no code item is gated later than G3 feature complete**; items marked IQ/OQ or PQ are site configuration (CC6), execution or documents.

**Who the humans are** (independent review PR-39). Changfu Kuo: owner, approver, PI of Study A, CGMH steward for the pilot, about 14 hours a week. Makoto: reviewer and Japan steward, about 14 hours a week. The safety-core engineer: part-time human engineer, one to two days a week through G3 and the named approver of every change to the six safety-core files, `authz` and the prefill path, named by design freeze. The CGMH independent second reviewer: hospital staff who co-sign validation and release records with the steward. An Aimedics operations person for orders, contracts, insurance, the pentest firm and on-site execution (to be named; until then Changfu). Agents build. Site IT, the trial offices, the privacy offices and counsel are the hospitals' and the vendor's own people.

**Review capacity rule** (PR-04). Human review is one reviewed pull request per work item per gate (about 90 pull requests to G3), with agent-to-agent review for the rest; human review concentrates on the safety core, the permission function, the pid map, the audit chain, the SQL generator, the prefill path and the migrations; the safety-core engineer approves those. Reviewer hours actually spent are recorded per pull request in the release record, so the GAMP claim is auditable. The capacity check is in §4.6.
