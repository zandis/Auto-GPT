## 1. Summary

TrialBox names almost every relevant standard somewhere. Few are met in full. Most rows are **partial** (a sound design, controls planned or half built) or **nominal** (the standard is named, no artefact exists). Nothing found here is a new patient-safety Blocker; the code-level safety and privacy Blockers are already in the register (#73) and are cited, not repeated.

Counts: **Blocker 0, Critical 3, High 13, Medium 14, Low 2** (32 findings).

The top five:

1. **REG-010 (Critical).** The IRB amendments (submitted 2026-11-10) and the IT security pack describe controls as facts that are not built: opt-out removal "within minutes", signed daily audit heads, "the administrator sees no patient data", break-glass, rate limits on every view. There is no list of these promises with their build status and no gate that ties real data to them.
2. **REG-007 (Critical).** The legal basis for screening identified records before consent is one sentence ("care relationship plus IRB approval"). It differs by regime (治験 under the GCP ordinance, research under the ethical guidelines, 臨床研究法; in Taiwan PDPA Art. 6, 人體研究法 Art. 12, the 2022 Constitutional Court judgment). The memo that must settle it is planned, not written.
3. **REG-014 (Critical, PoC).** The hosted demo accepts pasted chart text and file uploads, uses one shared password per role and has no acknowledgement gate. Nothing technical stops a clinician from pasting real patient text into a public cloud service.
4. **REG-001 and REG-002 (High).** Two Japanese rules change inside the project window: the ethical guidelines were amended on 2026-08-27 and apply from **2026-12-01** [W]; the GCP ordinance amendment for ICH E6(R3) is planned to apply from **April 2027** [W], one month before the Japan go-live. The repository cites the old versions and has no impact assessment.
5. **REG-021 (High).** The ODM export stamps every signature "21 CFR Part 11 electronic signature; ER/ES guideline (Japan)" while the register holds three open signature defects (CR-034, CR-035, CR-036) and TB-DS-16 says Part 11 does "not now" apply. A conformance claim is made in an artefact a sponsor receives, with no assessment behind it.

The non-device argument is sound in its core for both countries (screening, feasibility, EDC prefill). It is weakest at the planned treating-physician "own-patient view" with phenotype and eligibility layers (REG-016), at the product's own verdict words (REG-017, part of CR-078) and at the missing written views (REG-019).

For the PoC with two clinicians on synthetic data: three prerequisites (REG-014, REG-015, REG-013). A draft data-handling statement and tester acknowledgement is in §6.

**What would tip it into software as a medical device** (beyond TB-IU-01 §5, which is right):

- a tier or label that reads as a decision about a person ("eligible", "excluded", "abnormal", a traffic-light colour on a patient row);

- the "needs information" list worded as "order test X", which also prompts a pre-consent study procedure (TB-DS-11 H-15);

- the profile narrative offered as a handover or referral summary;
- sales text with accuracy, "finds patients others miss", 見逃し防止, or a comparison with physicians;

## 6. PoC data-handling statement and tester acknowledgement (draft)

### 6.1 Statement (give to each tester; one page)

**TrialBox proof of concept: how data is handled**

1. **What this is.** A demonstration copy of TrialBox, a research-support tool that screens records against study eligibility criteria. It runs on a hosted server at [region: Tokyo / Taiwan], outside any hospital. It is pre-release software. It is not validated and is **not a medical device**.
2. **The data in it is not real.** Every patient, chart, name, number and date in the demonstration is synthetic (made by a generator) or comes from public research data sets. No hospital's data is in it. Any likeness to a real person is chance.
3. **Do not enter patient information.** Do not type, paste, upload or photograph anything from a real patient's record, in any field, file, comment or feedback form. This includes names, chart numbers, dates of birth, note text, images and rare details that could identify a person. The demonstration blocks chart paste and uploads; the rule also covers free-text boxes and your feedback.
4. **Not for clinical use, not for trial conduct.** Do not use any output to decide on a patient's care, eligibility, approach or consent. Nothing here is a trial record, a screening log, a source document or an essential record under GCP. Results may be wrong; the demonstration uses a [stub / test] model setting.
5. **Your own data.** We keep: your name, role, institution, e-mail, account name, sign-in and action logs (time, page, action), and the feedback you give (written, spoken in calls, and screen recordings only if you agree each time). Purpose: to run the test, to keep the service secure, and to improve the product. Controller: Aimedics [legal name, address]. Contact: [name, e-mail]. We do not sell or share it. Named quotes are used only with your consent; otherwise feedback is used without your name.
6. **Where and how long.** The data is held on [provider, region]. If that is outside your country we tell you the country and its data-protection regime before you start (APPI Art. 28; PDPA Art. 8 notice). Logs and accounts are deleted 90 days after the test ends; feedback is kept without names.
7. **Your rights.** You may ask to see, correct or delete your data, or stop taking part, at any time, without giving a reason (APPI Arts. 33 to 35; PDPA Arts. 3, 10, 11).
8. **If something goes wrong.** If patient information is entered by mistake, tell [contact] the same day. We delete it, purge backups and logs that hold it, record the event without the content, and tell you what was done. You follow your hospital's own reporting rule.
9. **What your feedback is.** Design input for the product. It is not qualification (IQ, OQ, PQ) evidence and not research on you. If we later wish to publish findings from the test, we ask you first and ask the ethics committee whether review is needed.
10. **Confidentiality and interests.** The software and its screens are confidential until release. If you are an investigator at a pilot site or a reader in Study A, your part in this test is recorded in the study's conflict-of-interest file.
