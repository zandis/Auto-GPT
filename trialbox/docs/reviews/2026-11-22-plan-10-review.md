## The ten things that matter, in order

1. **The "13.8 % against 5 %" figure does not compare like with like, and no voting rule fixes it.** The public
   set's labels are noisy (the repository's own audit: 12 of 30 dropped "eligible" pairs were wrong labels, 10 were
   model errors); the figure is from a benchmark prompt, not from the product; the 5 % limit was written for clean
   physician-labelled sets. Three independent sweeps on the stored answers (the bench's, a worker's, mine) agree:
   every rule trades lost patients against caught ones along one curve. The loss is structural: about 2 % false
   blocks per criterion, times 11 criteria a trial. **The product-path figure the project's own decision D-918
   defines has never been run.** Section 2.1.
2. **Two engine faults on master can exclude a patient wrongly today.** The rule "a negated, family-history or
   suspected quote never excludes" exists on one of two evaluation paths only; and several real protocol lines
   compile to the wrong population with no flag (a pregnancy-test line becomes "sex = female", which excludes every
   man). Both are small to fix and matter more than any model change. Section 6a.
3. **Progress has stopped where it matters.** 78 of 271 in-scope cards are done; none of the 53 release criteria is
   fully met; pull requests reaching master per day went 58, 94, 37, 1, 7. No gate is on master. Section 1.
4. **The gate idea is right; the ratchet lists are not proof.** Ordinary code slips past the gate scanners (5 of 5
   attempts on gate 2, 8 of 10 on gate 5). A list of 925 places to patch measures typing, not safety. Re-scope each
   gate to the few real exits and one cross-path test. Section 3.2.
5. **"Complete release before the first site" cannot be reached as planned.** Seven release criteria depend on
   cards the adopted plan does not schedule; 193 cards remain; the first site adds 8 to 12 weeks of shadow running.
   Stage it: rc1 (demonstration), pilot-ready core, then 1.0. This is the owner's decision. Section 4.
6. **The safety net has holes that reviews keep finding and tests do not.** Every scheduled nightly since 11-10 is
   red; the mutation score of the safety core is 59.2 %, unchanged in five days; 47 strict expected failures keep
   known High defects green; the findings register has 63 open High rows and nothing newer than 11-19. Section 5.
7. **Masking in Japanese is not yet a claim the demonstration can make.** Names without a label, and names behind
   common encodings, pass the masker; log values that are not text are not scrubbed at all. Section 6b.
8. **Backup, restore and install were proved broken by the first real install, not by 20,900 tests.** The nightly
   backup failed every night on the installed box. Tests that are named after the runbook must run the runbook.
   Section 6d.
9. **One slow test stopped every merge for a day, and one integrator session is a single point of failure.** The
   test can be split with no loss in ten minutes of work. The integrator now also builds three tracks. Section 5.
10. **The bench host should prove things, not produce patches.** Its best uses this week: the product-path accuracy
    run, a full install-backup-restore-update cycle every night on a hospital-sized slice of the host, the 32 GB
    profile run as a product, encoding fuzzing of the masker, and killing mutants with tests. Section 7.

## 2. The bench's findings against the plan

The bench's public record is #1322. Each finding below says whether it is sound, whether plan 10 already holds it,
which card owns it, what changes, the next experiment, and what the demonstration may honestly say meanwhile.

### 2.1 "The shipped pair loses 13.8 % of eligible patients against a limit of 5 %"

**What was measured.** 1,700 public patient-trial pairs (1,200 labelled eligible, 500 excluded, 125 patients,
1,682 trials, 20,096 criteria). Each criterion is put to a model alone with a benchmark prompt. A pair is "lost"
when **any** criterion is blocked by **every** model in the rule; an unanswered criterion never blocks
(`tools/trec_ct_eval.py:360-378`) **[read]**.

**Is it sound? Six problems, each checked.**

1. **The labels.** The repository's own hand check (`eval/trec_ct/AUDIT.md`) read 30 dropped "eligible" pairs: 10
   model errors, 12 wrong public labels (for example a trial for male veterans, a 47-year-old woman labelled
   eligible), 5 criteria that belong to another arm of the trial, 3 ambiguous. Wrong labels alone are about 7.7 %
   of eligible pairs (Wilson 4.8 to 11.1 %) at that pair's 19.3 %. **A perfect reader would fail a 5 % limit on
   these labels** **[read, inferred]**. The scorer itself records that this set has no declared gold kind
   (`gold_kind.of_set("trec")` is `None`), and plan criterion 1 allows a bound only on a set with one.
2. **The path.** D-918 defines the validation figure on the product path (a screening run as a site, with the
   compiler, the product's prompt, quote checking, the confidence floor and the coded filters). The benchmark path
   has none of these. **No product-path figure is recorded anywhere in the tree** **[read]**.
3. **The number is not reproduced by the tree.** The stored answers of the stored pair, through the repository's
   scorer, give **17.4 % lost and 50.8 % caught** (209 of 1,200; patient-cluster interval 14.9 to 20.0). I rebuilt
   this from the raw files and the public labels and got the same figures **[ran]**. The 11-08 record's "9.4 %
   raw" is a projection (per-criterion rate, criteria per trial and an assumed share decided by coded filters), not
   a measurement **[ran, by a worker]**. So three figures exist for "the pair" (17.4, 13.8, 9.4) and #1322 does
   not name its verifier, prompt, rule or scorer. **R22-02, Medium, on open card #1322.**
4. **Noise between runs.** The same judge alone is reported at 35.3 %, 36.5 % and 35.9 % in the same record. A
   difference of one point between candidates (12.5 against 13.8) is inside that noise **[read]**.
5. **Two different "5 %" limits.** D-343 and D-918: the point estimate at most 5 % on at least 400 pairs. Plan
   criterion 1: the 95 % upper bound at most 5 % on at least 124 gold-eligible pairs from at least 12 protocols, per
   language. The bench calls it "the gate 5 limit"; gate 5 is the output gate. **R22-03, Low, on master (docs).**
6. **The scorer rewards silence.** A model that fails to answer never blocks, so it "loses" nobody: one stored
   candidate left 14,816 of 20,096 criteria unanswered and has the best "lost" in the table. **R22-04, Medium, on
   master**, `tools/trec_ct_eval.py:360`.

**What the stored answers do show** (my sweep, stored pair, no new inference) **[ran]**:

| Rule | Lost | Caught |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Judge alone | 34.8 % | 71.2 % |
| Both must block (the shipped rule) | 17.4 % (14.9 to 20.0) | 50.8 % |
| Both, and a third model | 14.8 % | 48.0 % |
| Both, and both cite evidence | 12.2 % | 39.6 % |
| Both, and the cited sentences overlap | 12.0 % | 39.4 % |
| Both, with the `negation` prompt | 17.9 % | 55.6 % |
| Three models, two prompts, all six block | 10.7 % | 44.6 % |
| All six, and evidence overlap | 6.8 % | 33.4 % |
| Only exclusions may block from notes (an inclusion "not met" goes to review) | 8.8 % | 30.0 % |
| A pair is dropped only on two blocking criteria | 3.7 % | 17.0 % |

- The three rows that use evidence read it through a parser that drops quoted evidence (section 2.5); they are
  understated and are shown for shape only.
- The bench's own sweep of 264 rules (comment 7076422454) and a worker's sweep reach the same shape: **no rule
  keeps the loss under 5 % and still screens.** The bench's reading "the gap is in the decision rule, not model
  size" is half right: it is not model size, and it is not the rule either.
- **It is arithmetic.** The pair blocks 1.97 % of criteria on eligible pairs; a trial has 11.3 criteria on
  average; independent blocks would give 20.1 %, measured 17.4 %. By number of criteria: 1 to 5, 9.3 %; 6 to 10,
  14.7 %; 11 to 20, 21.8 %; 21 to 40, 29.9 %. To reach 5 % at 11 criteria the per-criterion rate must fall to about
  0.45 %, four times lower. Trials over 40 criteria were left out of the set, so real protocols would be worse.
- **Of the 267 blocking criteria, 144 are inclusions answered "not met".** That matches the engine finding of
  section 6a: an inclusion "not met" stands on any quote, even one that says nothing about the criterion.

**Is it inside plan 10?** Partly. Criterion 1 and cards 9i-5, 10G-23, 10G-34, 10G-38 own the bound; D-1068 made it
"measured and reported, not a gate". Nothing in the plan owns the per-criterion rate, the product-path run, or the
rule for an inclusion "not met".

**Does it change scope or a milestone?** It does not move rc1 (rc1 claims no bound). It changes what the pilot must
prove and how the product should behave: decision 1.

**Next experiments, in order.**

1. **The product-path figure, once** (D-918; `tools/n12_screen.py`), at a named head, with the answered share.
   Needs the bench; a few hours.
2. **A 100-pair label check of the lost eligibles by a person who reads clinical English**, not by a model. The
   bench has queued a 100-case sample; who labels it matters more than who runs it.
3. **Per-criterion false-block rate by criterion kind** on the criterion-level set, where labels are physicians'.
4. **The inclusion rule** (an inclusion "not met" from notes needs a quote that names the concept, else review),
   scored on the stored answers by evidence ids, then on the product path.
5. **Stop sweeping vote rules.** Three sweeps agree. A third reader costs a third of the card for 2.6 points.

**What the demonstration may honestly say.** "On notes, the box excludes only when two different models agree and
quote the record; it has no measured bound on missed eligible patients in any language yet; on a public English
benchmark with noisy labels the raw figure is 14 to 17 %, of which the project's own check attributes about a third
to the models." It may **not** say "at most 5 %".

### 2.5 "Japanese evidence extraction is weak (F1 0.34 against 0.73)"

**Withdrawn by the bench itself while this page was written, and rightly** (comment 7077253140) **[read]**. The
benchmark's answer parser keeps an evidence item only when it is all digits (`tools/mlbench.py:277`; I read the
line). In Japanese the verifier mostly returns the quoted sentence with its number in front, which the parser
throws away. With a parser that also reads the leading number the bench reports evidence F1 of 0.94 in English and
0.91 in Japanese on label-matched criteria **[bench; not re-measured]**. This review's own worker had found the
symptom (no sentence kept in 110 of 174 correct Japanese answers; F1 0.96 where one was kept) and named the wrong
cause, because it read the same parsed files.

- **It was a measuring fault, not a product fault**, as far as is known: the product checks the verifier's quote
  word for word against the record and does not use sentence numbers. Whether any product path has a digits-only
  rule is asked of lane J and is **not verified** here.
- **What else it touches:** every stored figure that reads the evidence field through this parser, which includes
  the rows "both cite evidence" and "the cited sentences overlap" of the sweeps in 2.1 (the bench's, the worker's
  and mine). Those rows are understated for answers that quoted text; the rows that use labels only stand.
- **The lesson is the one of 2.1:** a benchmark figure was carried into a plan-level conclusion ("the Japanese
  evidence step is the weak point") without its method. **R22-04** covers the scorer; this is its sibling.
- Inside plan 10: criterion 1's "at least 25 % of note cells decided on the frozen Japanese set" remains the
  measure that matters for Japanese, and it is unmeasured on the product path.

## 3. Scope

### 3.1 Is "rc1 = supervised demonstration, eight gates before a pilot" still the right shape?

**Yes, with one correction** **[opinion]**. The root-cause finding is right and this review confirms it from four
directions: a rule kept at each entrance separately is a rule that one entrance forgets. Section 6a finds exactly
that in the engine (the guard on one path of two), 6b in masking (logs, mail, the bundle each scrub in their own
way), and the gate drafts' own reviews find it again.

The correction: **the engine is missing from the eight gates.** Gate 1 covers what the compiler releases. Nothing
covers the decision itself: "what may turn a model's answer into an exclusion" is implemented on two paths, and
they differ. Make it part of gate 1 or a ninth gate: one function decides a note answer's effect, for every
scenario.

### 3.2 Is the gate programme sized honestly?

**No. The number 894 (now 925) measures the wrong thing** **[ran, by two workers; read]**.

- **The lists are made by scanners that see known spellings.** On gate 2's branch, five of five ordinary ways to
  build a record key outside the gate passed its check. On gate 5's branch, eight of ten ordinary ways to write
  patient text to a sink passed its scan. The master ratchets behave the same: SQL built with a join of strings, an
  error decided by a process's error text, a model path split across a plus sign, all pass. **R22-05, Medium.**
- **Most entries are not exits.** A sample of 30 of gate 5's 360 entries: 5 carry patient text, 18 cannot, 7
  unknown. Real work is perhaps 60 to 145 entries, not 360.
- **One fix covers many entries.** 228 of the 360 are log lines. One formatter that never writes an unscrubbed
  value covers all of them at once, and that formatter has the fault of section 6b today.
- **The tests of the gate drafts let planted mistakes through:** on #1294, removing the national-identifier
  conflict check and dropping the source namespace from the key both stay green; on #1295, a partly masked name and
  a dropped refusal stay green. **R22-06, Medium, on open cards #1294 and #1295.**

**Recommendation.** A gate is done when (i) the function exists; (ii) **the runtime refuses the bypass** where it
can (the gateway is already the only model exit and a test enforces it: that is a real gate); (iii) one
**cross-path test** drives two synthetic people through every entrance and compares the result (for gate 2:
correction, merge, bulk import from two sources, the lake, OMOP, the first EDC draft); (iv) the list stays as a
tripwire with a corpus of known bypasses that each review adds to. "The list is empty" is not the criterion.

Gates 3, 6, 7, 8 are not started. Section 6c says which a pilot needs first.

### 3.5 What replaces the list: the cross-path test for gate 2, as a design

Asked for by the integrator after the first draft. It is a design, not built and not run **[opinion]**. Gates 4,
5 and 7 take the same shape with their own people, entrances and invariants.

**The idea.** The list asks "does any line still build a key by itself?", which a scanner answers badly. The test
asks the question the gate exists for: **"can two people ever share a record, or one person's record split, by any
road into the box?"** It does not read the code. It drives data through every road and compares what comes out.

**1. A small adversarial hospital (data, about 12 synthetic people).** Each person exists to collide with another:

| Pair | What collides |
| --- | --- |
| A, B | The same visit number and the same order number on different days |
| C, D | The same record id "1" from two source systems (two bulk imports, two namespaces) |
| E, F | Chart numbers that differ only by leading zeros, full-width digits or spaces |
| G | One person whose chart number is corrected; later a correction arrives for a record filed under the old number |
| H, I | Two charts merged into one; a record carried over, then corrected under the surviving chart |
| J, K | The same chart number with different birth dates, and with different national identifiers (must be held, not merged) |
| L | A person who opted out, then appears again under an alias |

The kit that writes specification-shaped SS-MIX2 storages and the bulk-import fixtures already exist
(`tests/ssmix2_spec_kit.py`, `tests/ssmix2_probes.py`); the people are a table, about 150 lines.

**2. Every road in (enumerated from the product's own source registry, not from a hand list).** SS-MIX2 full
night; SS-MIX2 with corrections, cancels and resends; bulk import from one source and from two; CSV; back-fill in
two batches across its commit boundary; the deletion path; a rebuild of the lake from empty against the
incremental path; the OMOP load and export; the first EDC draft and a second draft; the patient registry's alias
path. The test fails if the registry names a source kind the test has no road for, so a new connector cannot be
added without it.

**3. Every order.** Each scenario is run in at least three orders (as written, reversed, interleaved by person)
and twice in a row (idempotence). Order and replay are where the reviews found most identity faults.

**4. What it asserts (invariants over the published stores, read with SQL, not over function calls).**

- **No sharing.** No published row, in the lake, the OMOP tables, the vectors, the answer store or an EDC draft,
  is reachable from two different people.
- **No splitting.** Each source record of each person appears exactly once after any order of corrections,
  merges and replays; the count per person and type equals the table's expected count.
- **Path independence.** The incremental result equals a rebuild from empty, row for row, after keys are
  compared as sets.
- **Hold, do not guess.** J and K publish nothing for the conflicting chart, in every store, and a held state is
  visible to the steward.
- **Opt-out survives.** L's records are absent from every store under every alias, before and after a rebuild.
- **Stability.** A person's published key does not change between two nights unless a recorded correction says
  so, and then the old key is tombstoned everywhere.

**5. Planted mistakes prove the test, once, when it is written.** The six survivors of section 5.3 and the five
bypasses of section 3.2 are applied one at a time (the namespace dropped from the key; the conflict check removed;
a key built with an f-string in a new file). **Each must turn the test red.** That is the acceptance of the test
itself, and it is the answer to "what does it see that the list does not": a bypass that does no harm stays
green, and a bypass that mixes two people is caught whatever its spelling.

**6. Cost.** To write: about two to three days for one lane, most of it the roads that have no fixture yet (the
first EDC draft, back-fill across its boundary). To run: about 12 people times about 12 roads times 3 orders, each
a small ingest against the test database; an estimate of 3 to 6 minutes on a CI worker, so it can ride every
train in the unit tier **[inferred, not measured]**. A larger random version (hundreds of generated people with
generated collisions) runs nightly on the bench host.

**7. What happens to the 220 entries.** They become a work list read against the test, not a programme: an entry
on a road where the test is green and the planted mistakes are caught is closed by a sentence; an entry on a road
where the test is red is a fault with an input, and is fixed by moving that road behind the gate. The sample of
gate 5's list suggests about a third to a fifth of entries are real exits; expect the same order here.

**What it does not do.** It cannot prove a road nobody enumerated; the registry check of step 2 and the list as a
tripwire cover that. It says nothing about a real hospital's numbering habits: the site questionnaire and the
first reconciliation at the site (F2) do.

### 10.4 The rule that stops the scope growing again

1. **One page per ring**: does, does not, proved by, its cards. The owner confirms it by a decision; a confirmed
   ring is frozen.
2. **A frozen ring changes only for a fault in its own promise**: a finding that shows one of its "does" lines
   false, with an input. Everything else (an idea, a review's recommendation, a new standard) goes to the next
   unconfirmed ring.
3. **Only the owner adds to a frozen ring, and only by a swap** of something of equal size, in the same decision.
4. **Reviews file findings, not cards.** The integrator sorts each finding into "fault in the frozen ring" or
   "later ring". This review's 67 findings are to be sorted that way; 16 of them are ring 0.
5. **One ring is built at a time.** The next ring's page is written meanwhile and confirmed when the current ring's
   proof is recorded.
6. **Each train reports the current ring's count**, so growth is seen the day it happens.
