# Hostile review of `SPEC_v2.md` (TrialBox v2, "the Trial Agent")

Reviewer: adversarial review agent · 17 Oct 2026 · Inputs: `SPEC_v2.md` (1,290 lines, removed after this review; see git history at `c60ab4f`), `docs/LANDSCAPE.md`, `SPEC.md` (v1), and the v1 code at head `1115235`. Line numbers below are `SPEC_v2.md` unless stated. Resolutions: `SPEC_v3.md` Appendix G.

---

## 1. Verdict

**Not approvable as a build contract.** It is a well-written product vision with a credible deterministic core carried over from v1, but it fails five of the owner's eight binding requirements as written:

1. It makes a **web console with SSO/MFA mandatory for end users** (G1–G4 signatures, evidence viewer, Study Card, Data Atlas, configuration) and spends an R1 sprint task, an ADR, a Keycloak service and an NFR on it. The owner asked for no UI except for administrators.
2. It is **engineered for latency and for data-centre hardware** (acknowledgement ≤ 2 min, understanding ≤ 15 min, 99.5 % availability, 2× RTX PRO 6000 / H200 / B200 appliance, DGX B200 8-GPU reference portfolio, five concurrently served models, LiteLLM, Langfuse, Keycloak, OPA, DBOS, HAPI). The owner's candidates are a Mac Studio, a DGX Spark or one RTX 5090.
3. The **filter-first cascade is implied but not specified, measured or cost-bounded**: nothing caps LLM calls per run, nothing says a structured-decidable criterion must never reach the LLM, nothing dedups judgements across the 50-study portfolio, and FEAS headline tiers (`strong: 120`) silently require judging every note criterion for the whole population.
4. It keeps a **cloud-LLM path and a Hermes multi-channel agent** that the owner's on-prem rule and minimal-surface rule exclude, and OD-2 (line 1159) still contemplates **integration with a third-party screening vendor**.
5. **Multilingual handling is one line** (FR-AG-4: zh-TW or English) and one eval suite; mixed-language abbreviated notes, bilingual terminology normalisation, Japanese, and output-language policy are not specified, and §8.3 cites a v1 negation-cue list that does not exist in the code.

In addition several "where we are" claims are wrong or unverifiable (a `poc_status.md` that does not exist, "complementary suppression" that v1 never implemented, a `trialgpt/` directory that is not in this repository), several sprint tasks are not codeable as written (IR v2 `attribute` paths with no data model, DBOS exactly-once sends, MinIO Object Lock on a single-disk deployment), and the R1 exit criterion (real-data validation on ≥ 5 closed studies within 10 weeks) cannot be met before the IRB decision the spec itself schedules for week 4. Fix the findings marked **blocker** and the spec becomes a contract worth building against; the deterministic core (CQL≡SQL gate, quote-verified judging, hash-chained audit, byte-identical reruns) is genuinely good and should not be touched.

## 2. Findings (most severe first)

### F-01 · blocker · End users are required to use a web console (owner: no UI except admins)

**Where.** §1.3 line 77 ("a small web console for approvals, evidence and configuration"); P4 line 93–94; §3.1 lines 175–176, 184 ("never has to open the console unless a signature is needed", "by reply or console"); §3.2 line 198 (Console channel, R1: "Approvals with e-signature, evidence viewer, Study Card, Data Atlas, configuration"); lines 199–200 (Teams/LINE carry "links to the console"); §3.3 line 217 ("asks for the PI or CRC signature in the console"); §3.4 line 233; §4.2 line 270 (console container); §4.5; §5.6 line 553 (HTML routes `/studies`, `/approvals`, `/candidates/{study}`, `/atlas`, `/config`); FR-M4-4 line 648 ("console link"); FR-M4-5 line 649; §7 line 735 (gates set only by `POST /v2/gates/{id}/decision` after re-authentication); NFR-10 line 903 ("console tasks ≤ 3 clicks to sign"); NFR-11 line 904; SEC-3 line 942 (SSO + MFA for console); §12 line 1056 (`services/console`); §15.1 line 1122 ("console (approvals, Study Card, evidence)" in R1); S1-5 line 1137; Appendix D line 1259 (`STATE … G4 pending signature in console`); CMP-04 line 1217 ("Verified by: console e2e"); ADR-06 line 376; identity row line 286 (Keycloak).

**What is wrong.** Every human gate (G0 mapping, G1 criteria, G2 release, G3 candidate decision, G4 pipeline state) and every evidence view is routed through a browser UI with SSO. That is the v1 non-goal ("No UI", `SPEC.md` §1) reversed, and it is the owner's explicit requirement reversed. It also drags in Keycloak, MFA, Playwright tests, OWASP ASVS L2 for a web app, and a validated GxP UI, none of which the owner asked for.

**Failure it causes.** PIs will not log into another portal (the landscape doc itself says so, LANDSCAPE §7 white space 2); gates time out; the box reverts to "list delivered, nothing signed", which is the funnel leak the spec claims to fix (§14.3). It also doubles the validated surface.

**Fix (design).** Replace the console with **Sign-by-Reply**, a UI-less, Part 11-conformant signature flow, and keep a console only for the system administrator (health, queues, keys, update, model registry; no PHI):

- *Manifestation and linking (11.50, 11.70).* Every gate email carries a `gate_id`, the SHA-256 of the object being approved, the meaning text (from §7.1), and the exact reply line to send (`APPROVE G1 GZQO/v1.2.0 sha=ab12…`). The reply, once verified, is stored as a `signature` record (schema already in §5.1) with signer name, UTC time, meaning and record hash; the box returns a **signature receipt** email and the archive export (FR-M9-3) renders the manifestation in human-readable form.
- *Two identification components (11.200(a)(1)).* Component 1 = possession of the delegated hospital mailbox, proven by DMARC-aligned authentication of the reply (v1 SEC-4, already implemented and hardened per D-86). Component 2 = a **one-time code** that the box sends on receipt of the reply through a *second* channel (hospital TOTP app enrolled at delegation time, or an OTP pushed to the user's Teams/LINE account, or SMS via the hospital gateway); the signer replies once more with the code (or includes a TOTP in the first reply). Codes are bound to `(gate_id, record_hash, nonce)`, expire in 10 minutes, are single-use, and three failures lock the gate and alert the research office. Because each signing is a non-continuous session, both components are required every time (11.200(a)(1)(ii)); this is the conservative reading.
- *Identity verification before credentials (11.100(b)).* The delegation log (FR-M4 scope, Study Card `team[]`) is the identity record; the research office enrols the TOTP seed at delegation and the audit ledger records it. For FDA-regulated studies the hospital files the 11.100(c) certification letter; add this to CMP-04.
- *Higher-assurance option.* Taiwan's Electronic Signatures Act (amended 2024) gives certificate-based digital signatures the presumption of authenticity; physicians already hold the NHI 醫事人員卡 (HCA). Offer **S/MIME-signed replies with the HCA or a hospital-issued certificate** as the optional strong profile (the v1 mail gateway already supports S/MIME). This is still UI-less.
- *Each former console view, without a UI.* Study Card → `STATUS` returns a rendered PDF/HTML attachment (aggregate) with a "changed since last message" section. Evidence viewer → the candidate XLSX (v1 §9.2) gains a per-criterion evidence sheet (quote, date, note type, document id, assertion label) and, for `strong` candidates, a one-page PDF per patient; both encrypted, internal only. Data Atlas → the workbook round trip already in §3.4. Configuration → Git repository + `trialbox config show` for admins only. G3 → `CONFIRM`/`REJECT`/`DEFER` by reply as Appendix D already allows, plus the OTP. G4 → `STATE P0156 consented 2026-10-17` by reply plus OTP; drop "pending signature in console".
- *Quoted-text hygiene.* The reply parser must strip quoted text and signatures and must never read PHI back from the quoted candidate list; the v1 `In-Reply-To` matching (D-44) is the thread key.
- Delete S1-5, ADR-06, SEC-3's console clause, NFR-10's "console tasks", the Keycloak row (admins authenticate against hospital LDAP with a local MFA, no OIDC broker), and CMP-04's "console e2e" (replace with "Sign-by-Reply e2e").

### F-02 · blocker · The filter-first cascade is not explicit, measurable or cost-bounded

**Where.** §4.4 lines 330–333; §7.3 lines 771–782 ("for patients without hard failure: note_criteria: retrieve → judge → … verify"); FR-M3-2 line 631 (note sampling ≤ 200 patients — good); FR-M4-2 line 646 (portfolio screening, every patient × every study); AI-09 lines 858–859 (self-consistency k = 3); NFR-16 line 909 ("GPU budget per role enforced by the model gateway"); §5.5 line 521 (`headline: strong 120, potential 85, watchlist 30` in a FEAS packet).

**What is wrong.** The cascade is structured → notes, but: (a) nothing states the invariant *a criterion decidable from structured data is never sent to a model*; (b) "patients without hard failure" includes every patient with unknowns, so the LLM population is the whole scope minus hard exclusions, and no budget bounds it; (c) note criteria are not ordered by exclusion power or short-circuited (an exclusion-type note criterion that fires makes the remaining judgements wasted); (d) the 50-study portfolio (FR-M4-2) judges the same patient × the same canonical criterion once per study; (e) judge results are not cached across weekly runs even when the chunk set is unchanged; (f) v1 code still has the full-record fallback (`evaluate.py:34`, `FULL_RECORD_MAX_CANDIDATES = 100`, 28 k tokens each) which v2 inherits silently; (g) the FEAS headline tiers in §5.5 require `strong`/`potential` for the whole population, which contradicts the ≤ 200-patient sample in FR-M3-2 and would cost millions of tokens per feasibility; (h) there is no metric that says what fraction of the population reached a model.

**Failure it causes.** On a single-GPU box the weekly pre-screen will not finish in the night window; the cost/precision balance the owner asked for cannot be reported; FEAS packets cannot be produced as specified.

**Fix (text).** Add §4.4a "The cascade" as normative:

> C-1 Stages, in order, each recorded with its population count in the run manifest: **S0** scope and opt-outs (SQL); **S1** structured criteria (SQL; CQL verification sample); **S2** cheap note filters: for each note criterion, FTS/regex pre-check of the criterion's bilingual keyword set in the window — zero hits ⇒ `insufficient` without a model call; a deterministic negation/assertion pass on the hit sentences; **S3** `judge` (small local model) on the residue, note criteria ordered by historical exclusion power × cost, evaluated lazily with short-circuit on the first hard `not_met`; **S4** `verify_judgement` (second model family) only on candidates that would otherwise be `strong`; **S5** self-consistency only when a site-calibrated abstention rule (F-09) asks for it.
> C-2 Invariant: a criterion classified `structured` MUST NOT be sent to any model; a `note` criterion MUST NOT be judged for a patient who has a hard structured failure or an opt-out.
> C-3 Budgets: per study per run, `max_llm_items` and `max_tokens` from the ruleset manifest (defaults 2,000 items / 10 M tokens); exceeding the budget ends the run with a partial packet and a reason, never silently.
> C-4 Cache: judgements are keyed by `(pid, canonical_criterion_hash, chunk_set_hash, prompt_version, model_sha)`; identical keys are never re-judged across runs or studies; canonical criterion hashing (same atom/question across studies) is a compiler step.
> C-5 Metrics in every manifest and in the monthly QA report: `n_scope, n_after_S1, n_after_S2, llm_items, llm_tokens, llm_share = llm_items / (n_scope × note_criteria)`, and cost per candidate reviewed.
> C-6 FEAS headline tiers are estimates from the stratified sample with 95 % intervals (FR-M3-2); §5.5 `headline` becomes `{"strong_est": {"p": 0.065, "ci": [0.04, 0.09], "n": 120}}`.

Delete the full-record fallback from the batch path (keep it as an opt-in for ≤ 20 candidates).

### F-09 · major · False negatives are structurally silent

**Where.** §5.4 line 512 (`excluded`: "Never shown to the PI"); line 450–453 (`absence_of_evidence` default; coverage threshold unspecified); §7.3 line 776; FR-M4-8 line 652 (only rejection rate, i.e. precision, is monitored); §11.4 (recall only in the validation study).

**What is wrong.** A hard structured failure drops a patient forever; the only recall measure is the one-off historical-enrollee capture. Miscoded diagnoses, stale "history of malignancy" codes, local lab codes without LOINC maps, and departments with low coverage will exclude eligible patients, and nothing in production will ever notice. The `missing_data → unknown` rule depends on an Atlas threshold that the spec never sets.

**Fix.** FR-SAFE-1: each structured criterion gets a `filter_class` ∈ {hard, soft} set by the compiler from Atlas computability (hard only if coverage ≥ 0.9 for the domain in the scope *and* the atom is single-domain presence or a value comparison with ≥ 0.9 unit consistency); `soft` failures route the patient to S2/S3 instead of dropping. FR-SAFE-2: **monthly recall audit** per recruiting study: sample 50 patients excluded only by hard structured criteria, run the note pipeline (and, for 10 of them, a CRC question by email), estimate the false-exclusion rate with a Clopper–Pearson 95 % interval, and demote a criterion to `soft` when the upper bound exceeds 10 %. FR-SAFE-3: "history of" exclusions default to a window and an `evidence_of_absence` requirement for cure/resolution (e.g. malignancy within 5 years unless a remission note exists). FR-SAFE-4: the coverage threshold for `missing_data` is a named parameter (default 0.8, per domain, per scope) recorded in the manifest. Report FN estimates in the monthly QA report (CMP-02).

### F-10 · major · Small-cell protection is defeated by what-if differencing and per-investigator counts

**Where.** FR-M2-1/2 lines 621–622 (drop-one gains, threshold changes on the same snapshot); §5.5 line 520 (`alone_excludes`, `drop_one_gain`), line 526 (`investigators: candidates: 64` per PI), line 528; FR-M3-7 line 636; FR-M3-9 line 638 and FR-M11-3 line 715 (standing approvals for machine requests); PRIV-2; `tb.lake.aggregate` line 569 (parameterised templates callable from workflows).

**What is wrong.** Controlled rounding protects one table. A sequence of what-if runs (`WHATIF drop INC-05`, then `set INC-05 eGFR=29`, `=28`, …) or a standing federated endpoint lets a requester difference rounded totals and recover cells below 5, and the per-investigator candidate counts in small clinics identify individuals by schedule. Opted-out patients are also "counted in aggregates" (FR-M4-6), which may exceed the scope of a research opt-out under PDPA §6/§19–20.

**Fix.** PRIV-7: a **privacy accountant** per `(snapshot, study, requester)`: every aggregate released outside the research office consumes budget; what-if results are internal-only, limited to 10 variants per study per snapshot, and never G2-released without a fresh packet; machine/federated answers (M3-9, M11-3) add calibrated geometric noise (ε accounted per quarter per requester) on top of rounding; per-investigator counts are published only when ≥ 10; drop-one tables are released externally only at the rounded level and only once per ruleset version. PRIV-8: opt-out scope is configurable (`contact_only` vs `all_research_use`), default `all_research_use`, i.e. excluded from counts too.

### F-11 · major · Prompt injection and agent overreach through free-text intake

**Where.** §3.3 lines 205–207, 221–222; §4.5 line 339–340 (`tb.submit_request` callable by the gateway model); §8.1 `classify_intent`; §10.5; §3.2 Teams "simple replies in"; `redteam` gate line 873 ("0" attack success on a synthetic suite).

**What is wrong.** The `router` model reads the whole email (sender text plus quoted chains, HTML, signatures, forwarded sponsor mail) and emits a typed `request` with parameters that include scope, study and attachments; "acts only on what the authenticated sender wrote" (line 221) is not implementable on email, where quoted text is heuristically delimited. The guard model is probabilistic; a 0 % attack rate on a synthetic suite is a test, not a property. Teams replies have no identity binding to the delegation log.

**Fix.** Deterministic-first intake: (1) parse the v1 subject grammar; if it matches, no model is involved. (2) Otherwise strip quoted text (RFC-style `>` blocks, `On … wrote:`, `寄件者:` headers, Outlook separators) and run the classifier on the remaining text only; the classifier's output is a **proposal** rendered back as an exact grammar command; the sender must reply with it (or `OK`) before any workflow with external effect starts (make FR-AG-1 explicit about the reply, and exempt only `STATUS`). (3) Parameters that select recipients, scope beyond the sender's delegations, or external release are never taken from free text; they come from the delegation log and the Study Card (keep §10.5.5). (4) Teams/LINE are outbound only (F-05). ### F-12 · major · E-signature validity and Part 11 mechanics are asserted, not specified

### F-13 · major · Regulatory gaps (Taiwan HSRA/PDPA, IRB timing, NHI, intended-use boundary)

**Where.** §2.4 lines 152–158; §10.6; OD-6 line 1163 (IRB pathway decided week 4); §15.1 R1 exit criterion line 1122 (real data on ≥ 5 closed studies by week 10); CMP-14–17; CMP-15 "D" only; FR-M7-4 (CTCAE grading); FR-M5 (patient→trial for an individual).

**What is wrong.** (a) Pre-screening identifiable patients before consent needs an IRB approval or waiver under the Human Subjects Research Act and a PDPA §6 basis; R1 schedules real-data work weeks 1–10 while the IRB decision is "needed by week 4" and typical Taiwan IRB cycles are 1–3 months; the exit criterion is therefore unmeetable. (b) PDPA (amended Nov 2025, with the new Personal Data Protection Commission) requires a security maintenance plan and 72-hour breach notification; CMP-15 is verified by "D" with no workflow. (c) The NHI secondary-use opt-out applies to the NHIA database, not to the hospital's own claims copies; FR-M4-6 should say so rather than "where it applies". (d) The intended-use argument (not a device) is weakened by FR-M7-4 ("lab abnormalities graded by CTCAE thresholds … within 24 h") and FR-M5 (individual-patient matching on clinician request): both look like per-patient clinical analysis; TFDA's software classification guidance keys on intended use and risk, and the EU/FDA CDS exemptions require that the clinician can independently review the basis. CMP-28 must contain the written analysis for these two features, not just a gate. (e) Retention: ICH E6(R3) essential-record retention (sponsor-defined; EU CTR 25 years) and TFDA GCP record rules should be named defaults in PRIV-6, not "configurable". (f) E6(R3) §3 sponsor oversight: sponsors must be told that an AI tool pre-screens for their study; add a one-page sponsor disclosure template to the feasibility packet.

**Fix.** Make OD-6 a hard precondition: "No identifiable-patient run until IRB approval (umbrella or per study) is on file; R1 exit criteria use the hospital's **existing** de-identified research database (CGRD-type) for retrospective validation, and the IRB umbrella approval is a week-1 deliverable". Add FR-SEC-10 breach workflow (detect → contain → notify PDPC/MOHW within 72 h → patient notification) with a tabletop test. Add the CMP-28 written analysis for FR-M7-4 and FR-M5, and re-scope FR-M7-4 to "events already graded by the hospital's lab critical-value system; the box lists them, it does not grade".

## 4. Innovation proposals

Each proposal: what, why it is hard to copy, how to build, how to measure. All respect on-prem, filter-first, batch, no-UI, multilingual.

**I-1 · Cascade with recall certificates.** *What:* every pre-screen run publishes, per study, an estimated false-exclusion rate of the deterministic filter with a 95 % interval, from a standing monthly audit sample of excluded patients judged by the note pipeline and spot-checked by the CRC (F-09). *Why hard to copy:* cloud vendors do not run structured filters they must defend, and nobody publishes recall in production. *Build:* `filter_class` in the compiler from Atlas coverage; `RecallAuditWorkflow` (nightly, budgeted at ≤ 2 % of LLM items); Clopper–Pearson bound; demotion rule; a "recall" sheet in the PI packet. *Measure:* FN upper bound per study; criteria demoted per quarter; enrolled patients who were ever hard-excluded (target 0).

**I-2 · Site-calibrated abstention by split conformal prediction per criterion type.** *What:* instead of thresholds on model confidence (AI-09 rightly distrusts it), compute a nonconformity score per judgement (verifier disagreement, quote-assertion mismatch, retrieval margin, language-mix flag) and set the abstention quantile per site and per language stratum from CRC-adjudicated pairs so that the accepted judgements have ≤ 5 % error with coverage guarantees. *Why hard to copy:* needs per-site adjudication data and a batch loop; SaaS products calibrate globally. *Build:* `calibration/conformal.py`, calibration set grows from G3 reasons and the §11.4 sample; quantiles stored per `(criterion_type, language_stratum)`; `pending_human` when score > quantile. *Measure:* empirical error on held-out adjudications vs the 5 % target; abstention rate; CRC minutes per candidate.

**I-3 · "Five-minute review" active learning by email.** *What:* every week the box picks the ten most informative pending judgements (uncertainty × disagreement × expected effect on tier) and emails them to the CRC as yes/no questions with the quote; answers become labels for I-2 and for a per-criterion-type exemplar bank used as few-shot context (no fine-tuning in production, so G5 governs only prompts/exemplars). *Why hard to copy:* requires the UI-less email loop and the per-site exemplar store. *Build:* `ActiveLearnWorkflow`; exemplar bank in the rulesets Git with language tags; retrieval of exemplars by criterion similarity (bge-m3). *Measure:* PPV lift per 100 labels; time to reach the ≤ 5 % error target at a new site.

**I-4 · Bilingual evidence spans and a self-growing site lexicon.** *What:* canonical text with stable offsets, sentence-level language tags, a lexicon that mines `ABBR (中文)` / `中文 (EN)` patterns and lab-dictionary pairs, and renders every evidence line as `code — 中文 (English)` (F-07). *Why hard to copy:* US vendors have no Taiwan mixed-text lexicon; it accrues per site. *Build:* `vocab/site_lexicon.parquet`, `textproc.canonical()`, `assertion.py` with bilingual cue lists, G0 review of the lexicon diff. *Measure:* quote verification rate; retrieval recall@5 per stratum; judge sensitivity gap between mixed and English strata ≤ 5 points.

**I-5 · Counterfactual relaxation with site-available safety proxies.** *What:* for each criterion, Trial Pathfinder-style pool gain plus the change in site-observable 90-day outcomes (ED visits, admissions, lab excursions) among the patients the relaxation would add, reported as rounded aggregates with safety-critical criteria locked. *Why hard to copy:* needs longitudinal EHR data inside the hospital; sponsor-side simulators only have claims or trial data. *Build:* `optimise.py` over the lake; outcome proxies as standard ValueSets; privacy accountant (F-10) applied. *Measure:* sponsors that change a criterion after a TrialBox report; pool gain realised.

**I-6 · Sign-by-Reply.** *What:* the Part 11 two-component e-signature over email with OTP/TOTP or HCA S/MIME (F-01/F-12). *Why hard to copy:* every competitor sells a portal; a UI-less signed workflow is a product philosophy, not a feature toggle. *Build:* `signing/` module, OTP channel adapters, receipt template, archive rendering, certification-letter template. *Measure:* time from packet to signed decision; share of gates signed within 48 h; QA audit findings = 0.

**I-7 · Questionnaire autofill with answer memory and change tracking.** *What:* per-sponsor template maps learned once, a canonical site-profile ontology (TransCelerate SIP), reuse of previously approved answers across sponsors, and a "what changed since your last answer to this sponsor" sheet. *Why hard to copy:* needs the site's own history and the on-site profile; sponsor platforms see only their own questionnaires. *Build:* `questionnaire_map` learning with CRC confirmation (exists in spec) + `answer_memory` table keyed by SIP field with provenance and validity dates. *Measure:* % of questions auto-answered; CRC minutes per questionnaire (baseline 4 h); answer-reuse rate.

**I-8 · Federated feasibility with a privacy accountant and noisy aggregation.** *What:* alliance queries answered by each box with calibrated geometric noise and controlled rounding, ε budget per requester per quarter, results merged at the alliance root by email (v1 COHORT MERGE) with noise shares that cancel in the sum (each member adds a share of a zero-sum random vector agreed pairwise). *Why hard to copy:* TriNetX-style networks hold the data centrally or trust the vendor; this is sovereign and auditable. *Build:* `privacy/accountant.py`, `privacy/noise.py`, pairwise seeds exchanged once at alliance onboarding. *Measure:* ε spent per quarter; relative error at n ≥ 50; zero releases below budget.

**I-9 · Portfolio screening with canonical-criterion dedup and judge caching.** *What:* criteria across studies are canonicalised (same atom/question → one hash); a patient × criterion × chunk set is judged once for all studies and all runs until the data changes (F-02 C-4). *Why hard to copy:* needs a compiler that owns criterion semantics; LLM-first products judge per study. *Build:* canonicalisation in the compiler; judge cache in Postgres keyed by content hashes; invalidation on snapshot delta per patient. *Measure:* LLM items per patient per week vs naive; cache hit rate (target > 80 % after week 4).

**I-10 · Event-driven watchlist with time-to-eligibility.** *What:* each watchlist item carries the computed date the blocking criterion can flip (washout end, next scheduled lab, next visit, age threshold) and is re-evaluated exactly then, with the PI told "becomes eligible on 12 Nov; next visit 14 Nov". *Why hard to copy:* needs the temporal IR and appointment data. *Build:* `flip_date` from IR v2 windows and the SoA; scheduler keyed by date, not cadence. *Measure:* watchlist → strong conversions; candidates presented within 14 days of their flip date.

**I-11 · Hierarchical Bayesian enrollment forecasting calibrated against the site's own history.** *What:* conversion rates (approach, consent, screen-fail) per therapeutic area shrunk to site-wide priors; posterior predictive p10/p50/p90; the PI's own guess recorded and scored (Brier) so the research office sees who is calibrated. *Why hard to copy:* needs the site's closed-study history inside the box. *Build:* `forecast/hier.py` (PyMC or a conjugate Beta-Binomial hierarchy); `site_history` from M6/M9. *Measure:* calibration slope and interval coverage after 10 studies; Brier vs PI forecasts.

**I-12 · Self-building validation sets from historical enrollment logs.** *What:* at install, the box ingests the CTMS/IRB enrollment logs of closed studies, reconstructs the lake as of each study's recruitment start (snapshot-as-of queries), runs the frozen ruleset and produces the historical-enrollee capture report and a criterion-level adjudication sample automatically. *Why hard to copy:* needs temporal snapshots and the approved rulesets on site. *Build:* `validation/replay.py`; as-of partitioning in the lake (already per-snapshot); CTMS identity link through the pid map. *Measure:* days from install to first validation report (target ≤ 14).

**I-13 · Overnight large-model second opinion.** *What:* because latency does not matter, the verifier role runs a model two to four times larger than the judge (e.g. a 100B-class MoE on the DGX Spark, or a 70B-class on a 128 GB Mac) in a separate night pass over only `strong` candidates and disagreements; one model resident at a time. *Why hard to copy:* interactive products cannot afford it; batch makes it nearly free. *Build:* model-pass scheduler (F-04 NFR-18); registry entries per pass; manifests record both SHAs. *Measure:* PPV of `strong` with vs without the pass; GPU hours per week.

**I-14 · eCRF pre-fill with source packets for SDV.** *What:* every pre-filled value ships with a one-page source packet (lab line, note sentence with quote span, document id, date) so a monitor verifies in minutes; critical fields flagged; CDASH/ODM export. *Why hard to copy:* EHR→EDC vendors assume FHIR from a major EHR; this works from the HIS lake and bilingual notes. *Build:* `abstract_field` with lineage (exists); packet renderer; CDASH mapping tables. *Measure:* SDV minutes per visit; field agreement ≥ 0.95 structured.

**I-15 · NHI-aware recruitment (unique wedge).** *What:* use NHI prior-authorisation outcomes and renewal timelines as phenotype signals (a patient whose biologic renewal is failing is a candidate for a trial of an alternative), and compute the standard-of-care vs research billing split from the SoA at feasibility time so the budget is right before the CTA. *Why hard to copy:* no competitor models NHI; v1 already has NAV/TWPAS. *Build:* `claim` atoms in IR v2 with renewal windows; SoA → NHI fee-code mapping (FR-M7-2) moved to feasibility. *Measure:* candidates surfaced from NHI signals that enrol; budget-table acceptance by the research office.

## 5. Hardware view

**Workload shape.** After the cascade, a 300-recruiting-study, 20 k-scheduled-patient week should produce on the order of 10³–10⁴ LLM items per night (2–3 k-token prompts, ≤ 300-token outputs), plus a few protocol extractions (≤ 150 pages, 30–60 k input tokens).
