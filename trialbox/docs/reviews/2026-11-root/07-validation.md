## Summary (one page, plain words)

**The owner is right: the validation is not good enough.** [O] What exists is a large, careful *test* effort and a
large *plan*. What a hospital's quality office or a sponsor's auditor asks for is different: executed, dated,
reviewed records that show the shipped system does what its intended use claims, with numbers whose uncertainty is
stated in advance. Of that, almost nothing exists yet.

**What is good enough today.** [O, on the evidence below]
- The unit and console tests are many (8,734 test functions in `tests/unit` and `tests/console` [M]), typed, sharded,
  on a real PostgreSQL by default, and the pull-request tier runs in about 23 minutes [M].
- The safety rules ("unknown never excludes", "a proposed fact never counts") are written as tests and property
  tests, and the code that holds them is mutation-tested every night [R `tools/mutation.toml`].
- The audit trail, erasure, masking, ASVS and install were each *measured* this week by an honest card that reported
  failures as failures [R the five measurement pages of 2026-11-17]. That habit is the right foundation.
- Spec v4 section 16 already describes most of a proper programme: blinded double annotation with kappa, a calibration
  programme, replay of ten closed studies, pre-registered analysis scripts, IQ/OQ/PQ generated by command [R].

**What is not good enough.** [M and R as marked in section 1]
1. **No validation record has been executed.** `eval/qualification/` holds no record; `docs/validation/release-records/`
   holds only its README; no IQ, OQ or PQ protocol exists (`protocols/` is "planned") [R].
2. **Both nightlies are red every night.** The deployed-shape nightly (`trialbox-e2e.yml`) failed on all 8 runs
   listed, 2026-11-11 to 11-17; the quality nightly's mutation score is **59.3 %**, under its own floor of 60 and far
   from the 90 % target; the code that decides SCREEN's verdicts (`evaluate.py`) scores 45.1 % and the judge
   wrapper 42.9 % [M]. Plan 8 criterion 3 asks for three green nights; there have been none.
3. **No accuracy figure is on what ships, and none could prove "no eligible patient excluded".** The confirming
   launch P2b excluded 4 of 420 eligible pairs (5 of 424 as stored) [R `p2b-review` section 1]. Even zero of 420
   would only bound the miss rate under 0.71 % pooled, and under 7.2 % for the 40 SIGIR pairs [M]. The Japanese set
   (jmed, 330 eligible) asked the model **no** note question [R `p2b-review`, "Not shown"]; the Chinese and Japanese
   criterion gold is a **machine translation** of 53 English notes, "not clinician-verified" [R
   `eval/criterion_ml/README.md`]. So there is no per-language evidence at all for zh-TW and almost none for ja.
4. **The answer key has been changed after looking at the failures** (key v2, the six "conflicted" pairs, the new
   age-line class) [R `eval/public_accuracy_key/README.md`; `p2b-review` section 1]. Each change may be right, but a
   regulator reads it as moving the goalposts. Nothing is frozen or pre-registered.
5. **The EDC module changes the regulatory position, and the position has not been changed.** TB-DS-16 says Part 11
   and the Japanese ER/ES guideline apply "not now", because "REDCap or the sponsor EDC holds regulated data" [R
   `16-regulatory-position.md` section 6]. Plan 9 makes the EDC a module of TrialBox and "the record of CRF data" [R
   plan 9 section 4a]. From that moment TrialBox ships a system of record, and Part 11-style controls must be shown
   by executed test, not asserted. The audit-trail card found 6 High findings this week [R `audit-trail-2026-11-17.md`].

**The five changes that matter most.** [O]
1. **Restate the accuracy targets as statistical bounds, per language, and pre-register them** before the next paid
   run: "the 95 % upper bound of the eligible-exclusion rate is at most 2 % in each of en, zh-TW and ja, and at most
   1 % pooled". That needs **149 eligible pairs per language with no miss (236 with one)** and 299 pooled [M,
   section 3.1]. "No eligible patient excluded" cannot be proved by any finite set, and with n = 299 a box whose true
   miss rate is 0.3 % fails a zero-miss rule 59 % of the time [M]: the target as worded invites relabelling.
2. **A frozen, clinician-labelled gold set per language**, labelled blind by two clinicians with an adjudicator,
   kappa reported, sealed by hash before any model sees it, used once per release and never for tuning. The owner's
   de-identified carve is the only realistic source of zh-TW cases; ja needs a partner or a paid labeller.
3. **Make the nightlies green and keep them green**, and make the mutation score a release criterion with a floor
   that rises (70 % now, 85 % at the release candidate, per file not pooled).
4. **Write and execute the IQ/OQ/PQ protocols** (spec v4 VV-80 is a requirement with no code: there is no
   `trialbox validate` command [R]), starting with the EDC module's Part 11 OQ: audit trail, signatures, access matrix,
   each as an executed, dated test record on the installed bundle.
5. **Three outside eyes**, each a cost needing the owner's yes: clinician labellers (the gold set), a reader study of
   the evidence sheet (do reviewers catch a wrong answer?), and a penetration tester; plus one regulatory reader of the
   intended use once the EDC module is in.

**What it costs** (section 5 has the detail) [O, estimates]: about 70 to 95 people-days of our own lanes; 25 to 40
GPU hours (US$70 to US$110 spot at US$2.74 an hour [R `qualify-plan` line 149]); clinician time of about 40 to 60
hours per language for 300 labelled pairs; a penetration test and a regulatory read priced by quote (not verified).

## 3. The model's validation (what the owner most likely means)

### 3.1 Sample size behind "no eligible patient excluded"

Exact one-sided 95 % Clopper-Pearson bounds, computed here [M, `b7_n.py`, section 9]:

| Target: miss rate at most | Pairs needed, 0 misses (95 %) | (99 %) | 1 miss allowed (95 %) | 2 misses (95 %) |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 5 % | 59 | 90 | 93 | 124 |
| 2 % | 149 | 228 | 236 | 313 |
| 1 % | 299 | 459 | 473 | 628 |
| 0.5 % | 598 | 919 | 947 | 1,258 |
| 0.1 % | 2,995 | 4,603 | 4,742 | 6,294 |

The arithmetic: with 0 misses in n, the bound p solves (1 - p)^n = 0.05, so n = ln 0.05 / ln(1 - p), about 3/p (the
"rule of three"); with misses allowed it is the binomial tail.

What today's sets bound [M]:

| Set | Observed | 95 % upper bound of the miss rate |
| --- | --- | ---: |
| P2b pooled, as stored | 4 of 420 (without the six) | 2.17 % |
| P2b pooled, without the age-line class | 2 of 418 | 1.50 % |
| P2b sigir | 4 of 40 | 21.4 % |
| PoC-1 / P2b tcga | 0 of 50 | 5.8 % |
| jmed | 0 of 330 | 0.90 % (but no note question asked: not a model figure) |
| qualify pair gate minimum | 0 of 20 exclusions wrong | 13.9 % |
| selftest per language | 18 cases | 15.3 % even at 18 of 18 |
| qualify `n12` | 5 % of 400 allowed | 7.2 % |

**What this means.** [O]
- "No eligible patient excluded" is an observation, not a property. It must be restated as a bound. Proposed: per
  language, the 95 % upper bound at most **2 %**; pooled at most **1 %**; reported with the count of misses.
- A zero-miss rule is also a poor *test*: with n = 299 and a true rate of 0.3 %, P(0 misses) = 0.41, so a good box fails
  59 % of the time; at 0.5 % it fails 78 % [M]. That pressure is what produced key v2, the six conflicted pairs and the
  age-line class. Allowing one miss at n = 473 for the 1 % bound is fairer and still strict.
- **Clustering.** Pairs from one protocol share its compile; with an intra-protocol correlation of 0.05 and 10 eligible
  pairs per protocol the 299 becomes about 434 [M, design effect 1.45]. Draw eligible pairs from many protocols.
- **The qualification thresholds disagree with the release target.** The pair gate passes a model at 0 of 20, and
  `n12` allows 5 % lost [R `model-change-protocol.md` section 3]. Qualification should be a smaller version of the same
  bound (for example 0 misses in 149 per language), so a qualified model is one expected to pass the release.

**Per criterion class.** The P2b misses each came from a different class: an age line (coded), a sex-only atom on a
pregnancy line (coded), a note line (S3) [R `p2b-review` section 1]. Report per class (demographic, coded diagnosis,
lab value with time window, medication, note-only, temporal) with counts; gate only per language and pooled, since
per-class gating multiplies the n by six (about 900 eligible pairs per language) [O].

### 3.3 Gold-standard quality

- **Who labels.** Spec 16.8 already says: two independent CRCs or research nurses, an investigator adjudicates,
  qualification at 0.85 on 50 SIGIR pairs, Cohen's kappa at least 0.80 per stratum, blind to model output [R]. None of
  it has been done: no annotation guideline file, no annotation mode (VV-61) [R]. [O] Keep the spec's rules; they are good.
- **Kappa needs its own n.** For kappa near 0.85 to have a 95 % lower bound near 0.75, about 150 to 200 items per
  stratum are typical [O, from the usual large-sample variance of kappa; not computed here]. The 300-pair language set
  covers it.
- **Leakage.** SIGIR's patients are in `eval/criterion_ml` (prompt and bake-off work), in `judge_set` design, in P1b and
  in P2b, and the key was revised from P2b's failures [R]. It is a development set now and can never again be a test
  set. GMLP principle 4 [S].
- **Key changes after results.** Every revision (v1, v2, conflicted, age-line class) was argued in writing, which is
  to the team's credit [R]. The fix is procedural: freeze the key, then run; a later correction is a new key used on
  the *next* frozen run, never the one it was found on [O].
- **The synthetic hospital's limits.** Notes are generated from templates ("notes are the same zh-TW templates") [R
  `21-synthetic-hospital-spec.md` section 2]; the masking card found its planted forms are "this card's estimate of
  how hospitals write" [R `masking-leaks` verdict]. [O] The synthetic hospital proves plumbing, scale and planted rules;
  it can prove nothing about accuracy on real notes, and no accuracy figure from it should be printed beside one from
  clinicians.

   script hash, the targets and the decision rule, committed **before** the run; for the owner's carve, the manifest of

### 3.6 Drift, shadow mode, and site acceptance

- Shadow (evaluation) mode is a product feature, and plan 8 says it "is not the way we prove the product" [R plan 8
  section 2]. [O] Correct for release; but it is the right instrument for two things the release cannot prove: the site's
  own acceptance (its patients, its notes, its staff), and drift after install. Spec 16.11 already sets the KRIs
  (known-positive recall lower bound at least 0.90 on at least 59 enrollees; elusion bound per stratum; override rate
  in control limits) [R].
- The recall ledger's confidence sequence is built and simulated (miss coverage 0.003 against 0.097 for repeated
  Clopper-Pearson at p = 0.02) [R `eval/suites/recall_cert/README.md`]. [O] It is the strongest piece of model
  validation in the repository; it needs (a) a site sampling plan in the acceptance protocol (40 excluded patients a
  month is the simulated design) and (b) the planted-miss and 1,000-seed parts the README lists as missing.

### 3.7 Adversarial and injection suites

70 cases over 23 bases (en 7, ja 5, zh-TW 11), attacks and 13 benign controls; 0 changed facts required; run against
the stub in CI and against the pair in qualify [R `eval/injection/cases.yaml`; `qualification-made-ready` row 8].
Never run against the real pair in full [R]. [O] Adequate in design; add (a) attacks inside protocol text (the
compiler reads protocols from outside the hospital) per language, (b) attacks in full-width and mixed scripts, (c) a
count large enough that "0 of n" means something (59 attacks per target gives a 5 % bound).

### 3.8 Failure modes: where a wrong answer hurts

| Failure | Who is harmed | Detectable by a person? | Validation it needs [O] |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Eligible patient excluded (H-01) | the patient (a trial option not offered), the trial (slower accrual) | **no**: an excluded patient is not on any list | the bound of 3.1, plus the recall ledger at the site |
| Ineligible patient listed (H-03 path) | staff time; at worst an ineligible enrolment if the PI trusts the sheet | yes, by the reviewer | the reader study (3.9) |
| Wrong evidence quote shown (H-02) | trust; a reviewer misled | partly | quote verification is exact (`quotes.py` 100 % mutation) [M]; the reader study |
| Wrong prefilled CRF value (H-05) | data integrity of the trial | yes, at signature, if the reviewer checks | per-field agreement on a frozen set of synthetic CRFs; the reader study on the prefill page |
| Masking leak (H-04) | privacy | no | 113.9 leaks per 1,000 planted identifiers today [R `masking-leaks`]; the plan 9 criterion 15 detector |

The asymmetry is the point: the harm nobody can see (an exclusion) needs the statistical bound; the harm a person can
see needs proof that the person does see it.

### 3.9 Human factors: does the reviewer catch a wrong answer?

GMLP principle 7 asks for the performance of the human-AI team [S]. Proposed design [O]:
- 6 to 8 readers (CRCs and investigators, two languages at least), each reviews 40 evidence sheets from the synthetic
  hospital, in random order, of which about 25 % carry a planted wrong verdict (wrong quote, quote that does not
  support the verdict, stale date, wrong lab unit).
- Primary measure: the share of planted errors caught. To show a catch rate of 90 % with a 95 % lower bound at or
  above 80 %, about **60 planted errors** are needed (54 of 60 gives 81.2 %) [M]; 8 readers x 40 sheets x 25 % = 80
  planted, enough per site language pooled.
- Secondary: time per sheet, false alarms on correct sheets, which error kinds escape. A finding that readers do not
  catch a kind becomes a design change, then a second round.
- Cost: about 2 hours per reader plus our preparation (section 5).

### 3.10 Calibration and abstention

- Calibration: spec VV-40 to VV-43 describe a public prior, site certification by Learn-then-Test on disjoint folds,
  and monthly re-certification [R]; none is built (`calibration/` absent) [R]. [O] For 1.0, the honest minimum is a
  reliability table (stated confidence decile against observed agreement) per language on the frozen sets, and
  *no* threshold that accepts a judgement on confidence alone; the two-model rule plus "unknown never excludes" already
  routes doubt to a person.
- Abstention: "unknown" is the box's abstention, and a high abstention rate is a cost, not a safety failure. Report it:
  tcga left 68 to 75 of 500 open; jmed left 417 of 594 open [R `poc-1-evidence.md`]. Pair a miss bound with an
  "open share" figure so the box cannot pass the safety bound by abstaining on everything [O].
