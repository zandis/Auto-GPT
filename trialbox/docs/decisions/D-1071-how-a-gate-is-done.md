# D-1071 A gate is done by its function, a runtime refusal and a cross-path test

- **Task:** an owner's ruling, numbered by the integrator; the gate cards (lane G)
- **Date:** 2026-11-22
- **Status:** accepted (the owner's ruling of 2026-11-22 10:40 UTC; recorded here the same day)

## Ruling

1. A gate is done when three things hold: its one function exists; the running product refuses the bypass where it
   can; and one cross-path test drives synthetic people through every entrance and compares what is published.
2. The lists of places that do the job without the gate stay as tripwires, each with a corpus of known bypasses
   that every review adds to. "The list is empty" is not the criterion.
3. There is no programme of patches over the lists (925 entries on the day of this ruling). The local coder does no
   ratchet-list work.
4. A cross-path test is accepted when the planted mistakes and bypasses known for that gate each turn it red.

## Why

The scanners that make the lists see known spellings: ordinary code passed gate 2's check five times of five and
gate 5's scan eight times of ten, and a sample of gate 5's list found about one entry in six that carries patient
text. A test over the published stores asks the question the gate exists for, whatever the spelling.

## What it changes in earlier records

The root-cause plan of 2026-11-20 (the eight gates, relayed on #639 in comment 7052603473) stands for which gates
exist and in what order. Its way of finishing a gate, "the ratchet falling to zero", is superseded by points 1 to 3.
